================================================================================
PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION — TEMPERATURE INSERTION BY TERRAIN CLASS
Cross-variable RMSE response (U500, V500, Z500, Q700) stratified by terrain
class: Land Low (<300 m), Land Mod (300-1500 m), Land High (>1500 m),
Ocean, Polar (|lat|>60 deg)
Models: Aurora, FourCastNet (FCN), Prithvi-WxC, GraphCast
Source plots: fig_terrain_t_ins_rmse_improvement_bar.png,
  fig_terrain_t_ins_rmse_timeseries.png (one copy in each model folder)
Prepared from table_t_ins_terrain_summary.csv and the per-model
  intermediates/*_t_ins_terrain_rmse.csv caches.
================================================================================

0. EXPERIMENT DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Temperature is inserted (forced to ERA5 truth) and the response of U500,
V500, Z500, and Q700 is measured, stratified into five terrain classes
spanning a genuine orographic-complexity gradient (Land Low -> Land Mod ->
Land High) plus two non-orographic reference classes (Ocean, Polar). This is
the terrain analogue of the cloud-regime breakdown in the companion tcc
report and of the latitude-band breakdown in common/lats_FINAL/PHYSICAL_ANALYSIS.txt.
Unlike the purely zonal lats_FINAL bands, terrain class mixes latitude,
surface type, and orographic complexity together by construction, so
results here should be read as "where," not "how far poleward."


1. PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION OF MODEL BEHAVIOUR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 A consistent, model-spanning signature: temperature insertion helps the
    MASS field (Z500) more over mountains than over ocean or the pole.

Aurora's Z500 mature-phase improvement rises from Ocean (54.5%) and Polar
(52.3%, the two weakest) through Land Low (68.4%) and Land Mod (73.5%) to
Land High (72.5%); FCN's shows the same ordering (Ocean 19.9% and Polar
38.2% are not the simple low end, but Land High at 38.3% and Land Mod at
41.4% remain among the strongest). Prithvi's Z500 improvement is elevated
over land generally but is NOT monotonic with elevation and does NOT peak at
Land High: Land Low 58.6%, Land Mod 61.0% (Prithvi's actual peak), Land High
47.2% — its lowest land value, on par with Ocean (49.8%) and Polar (49.2%).
Two of four models (Aurora, FCN) therefore show their strongest temperature-
to-geopotential coupling over complex/elevated land generally (though
Aurora's own peak is Land Mod, not Land High, by a hair); Prithvi instead
shows its BEST land-class Z500 result at Land Mod and its WORST land-class
result at Land High — the opposite of what an earlier version of this
report concluded (see Section 1.2 for why this matters alongside Prithvi's
V500 finding, and Section 4/6 for the corrected model ranking).

Two compatible physical readings are worth distinguishing. First, the
control (uncorrected) RMSE baseline is itself larger over Land High than
over Ocean for Aurora (1614 vs 1250 m^2 s^-2) — a meaningful part of any
percentage-improvement metric over complex terrain reflects there simply
being more (uncorrected) error to remove, some of it likely
representativeness/resolution error from comparing a forecast grid cell to
a point or a smoothed orographic mass field, rather than a deeper dynamical
effect. (For Prithvi specifically, the baseline is actually comparable
between Land High and Ocean — 91.5 vs 95.6 m, using the corrected control
run — so this baseline-magnitude effect does not explain Prithvi's terrain
pattern; see Section 1.2 for what does.) Second, and not mutually exclusive,
mountainous terrain is a region where temperature is an unusually
informative variable precisely because elevated terrain heating drives
thermally-direct local circulations (mountain-valley flows, elevated heat
pumps) and strong local hydrostatic height anomalies that a dry hydrostatic
correction can capture cleanly, whereas over the open ocean and at the poles
the geopotential field is comparatively more controlled by remote,
advected, large-scale dynamics that a local temperature correction cannot
fully supply. Both readings point the same direction for Aurora (more correctable
local error over complex terrain), but only the second is a genuine
dynamical effect — a caveat to bear in mind before treating the largest
percentage gains as the most dynamically meaningful ones.

1.2 Prithvi's mountain weak point: Land High is a compound failure — both
    its weakest land-class Z500 AND its only negative V500, in the same
    terrain class.

Land High is Prithvi's weakest land class for Z500 (47.2% mature, below
Land Low/Land Mod and even Ocean/Polar, Section 1.1) and its V500 response
over the SAME terrain class is sharply negative at the Adjustment Phase
(-29.9%) and still negative at the Mature Phase (-6.9%) — the only terrain
class where Prithvi's V500 goes negative at all. Unlike an earlier version
of this report, which framed this as a strong Z500 result coexisting with a
localised V500 failure, the corrected data shows both fields are weak
specifically at Land High — a single, terrain-localised region where
Prithvi's temperature-insertion pathway underperforms across the board, not
a case of one field's success masking another's failure. This is still a
clean, terrain-localised illustration of balance-relation breakdown: the
physical reading is well supported by the orographic dynamics literature —
geostrophic balance is explicitly broken by orographic forcing, producing
strongly ageostrophic motion on the lee slope and generating mountain
(gravity) waves that redistribute momentum vertically (Wang et al., 2022,
Adv. Meteorology; gravity-wave-drag literature, Section 5) — exactly the
kind of locally ageostrophic, non-hydrostatic flow that a model relying on
a simple, geographically uniform thermal-wind heuristic would be expected to
handle worst precisely where real geostrophic balance itself is weakest;
the correction here is that this breakdown apparently extends to Z500 too
at Land High, not just V500.

1.3 GraphCast's second independent weak spot: complex terrain, not just the
    equator.

GraphCast's U500 over Land High is negative at BOTH phases (-5.7%
Adjustment, -28.5% Mature — the single worst cross-variable result for any
model/terrain/variable combination in this dataset). Its Z500 mature-phase
response is also negative over Land Mod (-33.2%) and Land High (-6.8%), and
mildly negative at the Pole (-16.0%), with only Ocean (10.8%) and, at the
Adjustment Phase, most classes staying reliably positive. Combined with the
sharp equatorial breakdown already documented for GraphCast-S in the
companion lats_FINAL report (Section 1.3 there), this shows GraphCast has
(at least) two independent dynamical blind spots — the deep tropics (low
Rossby number) and complex/mountainous terrain (strongly ageostrophic,
orographically-forced flow) — both regimes where the simple large-scale
geostrophic/rotational balance its icosahedral mesh seems to represent well
elsewhere breaks down in the real atmosphere too.

1.4 FCN's moisture failure, reproduced a fourth time, now with a
    terrain-specific twist.

FCN's Q700 mature-phase response is negative over Ocean (-6.2%) and
essentially flat/near-zero over Land Mod (1.3%) and Polar (-0.8%), with only
modest positive responses over Land Low (1.8%) and Land High (4.3%). This
again reproduces the moisture-specific failure documented in the companion
tcc and tuv_final reports, and adds the detail that the failure is, if
anything, WORST over the open ocean — the most spatially homogeneous
moisture surface of all five classes — reinforcing that this is a
field-level (moisture-specific), not a regional-complexity-specific,
limitation of FCN's temperature-to-moisture pathway.


2. REGIONAL AND SPATIAL ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Strongest improvements: complex/elevated land terrain generally, for
two of four models' geopotential field (Aurora, FCN — Section 1.1); Prithvi
is also strongest over elevated land but peaks at Land Mod, not Land High
(Section 1.1) — the clearest, most reproducible regional signal in this
dataset is "elevated land helps," not "the highest elevation class helps
most."

2.2 Strongest failures: GraphCast over Land High and Land Mod (Section 1.3);
Prithvi's Z500 AND V500 both specifically over Land High (Section 1.2);
FCN's moisture over Ocean (Section 1.4).

2.3 Hemispheric asymmetry: terrain class, as defined here, does not split
by hemisphere (Land High includes the Andes, Himalaya/Tibetan Plateau,
Rockies, and East African highlands together; Polar pools both poles), so
this dataset cannot resolve a Northern-vs-Southern-Hemisphere asymmetry —
the same limitation noted for the latband metric in the companion
lats_FINAL report. What it resolves instead, and resolves clearly, is the
land-elevation gradient and the land/ocean/polar contrast.

2.4 Smooth large-scale flow regions vs complex terrain. Ocean is, in every
model, among the weakest-or-mid-table classes for Z500/U500 improvement
from temperature insertion (Section 1.1) — consistent with the ocean
surface being the smoothest, most large-scale-dominated regime, where a
local temperature correction has the least unique information to add
relative to what the large-scale circulation already determines. Complex
terrain (Land High) is, by contrast, simultaneously where two of four
models (Aurora, FCN) see LARGE gains (Z500) and where two of four models
see their WORST failures (Prithvi's Z500 AND V500 both, GraphCast's
U500/Z500) — i.e. complex terrain is a regime of both real opportunity
(Aurora, FCN) and real risk (Prithvi, GraphCast) simultaneously, exactly as
expected for a regime where the simplifying geostrophic/hydrostatic
assumptions that hold elsewhere are most likely to break down (Section
1.2-1.3). Notably, Land High is where Prithvi's mass-field pathway is
weakest of any land class, not strongest — the opportunity/risk split here
runs by MODEL more than by a universal "complex terrain helps mass fields"
rule.


3. EXPERIMENT-LEVEL INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 What temperature-only insertion reveals about terrain-dependence.

For Aurora and FCN, temperature insertion's benefit to Z500 is terrain-
organised in a way consistent with both a baseline-error effect and a
genuine "temperature is most informative where local thermal forcing
dominates" effect (Section 1.1). Prithvi's Z500 is also elevated over land
generally, but peaks at Land Mod and is weakest of all at Land High — i.e.
its terrain organisation is real but does not track elevation monotonically
the way Aurora's and FCN's roughly do. For GraphCast, temperature
insertion's benefit to Z500/U500 is instead terrain-organised around where
the model's mesh-based dynamics break down (Section 1.3).

3.2 Cases where temperature insertion helps one field while harming another,
    in the SAME terrain class.

A clean case in this dataset is Prithvi over Land Low: Z500 improves by
58.6% mature (its second-best land class, Section 1.1) while U500 is
degraded by -14.1% mature (Section 1.3b), in the identical terrain class,
from the identical insertion. A second, even more striking case is Prithvi
over Land High, where BOTH Z500 (47.2%, its weakest land class) and V500
(-6.9%, its only negative terrain class) underperform together (Section
1.2) — here the divergence is not one field succeeding while another fails,
but a single terrain class where the model's temperature-insertion pathway
is broadly unreliable across every cross-variable examined. Both cases are
strong evidence that "improves dynamical balance" and "improves agreement
with truth" are not the same thing here — the model's fields do not move in
a mutually consistent way even in terrain classes where individual fields
show a moderate-to-large percentage improvement, exactly the kind of
imbalance the companion reports' Sections on geostrophic adjustment theory
describe.

3.3 Error propagation from the inserted variable into others, by terrain.

Propagation is reliably positive (temperature insertion helps the other
fields) over land of all elevations for Aurora and FCN; moderate for Z500
but weak-to-negative for U500/V500 for Prithvi everywhere, and weakest of
all specifically at Land High, where even Z500 propagation drops off; and
unreliable in sign over land (especially Land High/Land Mod) for GraphCast.
Ocean and Polar are the two classes where propagation is weakest but most
uniformly non-negative across all four models — i.e. the safest, if least
exciting, terrain classes for this particular insertion.


4. MODEL-BY-MODEL COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AURORA: best — Z500 over Land Mod/Land High (73.5%/72.5% mature), the
model's strongest cross-variable result in this dataset. Weakest — V500
over Land Mod specifically (20.9% mature, its lowest entry), and Q700 over
Land Mod is the model's only near-zero/negative cell (-5.8% Adjustment,
recovering to 5.2% Mature). Global vs local consistency: broadly globally
consistent, organised cleanly along the land-elevation gradient with no
outright sign flips.

FOURCASTNET: best — Z500 over Land Mod (41.4% mature) and Land Low (39.0%
mature). Weakest — Z500 and U500 over Ocean lag well behind every land
class at the Adjustment Phase (0.9% and 1.1% respectively, though both
catch up substantially by the Mature Phase); Q700 is negative or flat almost
everywhere, worst over Ocean (Section 1.4). Global vs local consistency:
moderate terrain-dependence with a consistent land > ocean ordering for the
wind/mass fields, and the same isolated moisture weakness found throughout
this report series.

PRITHVI-WxC: best — a solid Z500 result over land generally, peaking at
Land Mod (61.0% mature, Section 1.1); Q700 is strikingly strong at the Pole
specifically (70.8% mature) but is otherwise weak-to-flat over land (6.9%
Land Low, -0.2% Land Mod, -0.9% Land High — essentially zero or mildly
negative over Land Mod/Land High). Weakest — Land High is a genuine
double failure: Z500 (47.2% mature, its lowest land value) AND V500 (-6.9%
mature, the only terrain class where Prithvi's wind field is actively
harmed rather than merely unhelped) both underperform there (Section 1.2).
Global vs local consistency: moderate LOCAL (single-terrain-class)
consistency that is clearly terrain-organised but not cleanly
elevation-monotonic; Land High stands out as a terrain-localised
dynamical inconsistency affecting more than one field at once.

GRAPHCAST: best — V500 responds reasonably and fairly consistently across
terrain (14-36% mature, no sign flips). Weakest — U500 and Z500 over Land
High/Land Mod, both negative at the Mature Phase, the worst and second-worst
terrain-class results for any model/variable pair in this dataset (Section
1.3). Global vs local consistency: the least terrain-consistent model —
strong over ocean/polar, unreliable over complex land terrain, mirroring its
strong-extratropics/weak-equator split from the companion lats_FINAL report.


5. ARCHITECTURE AND TRAINING INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Prithvi-WxC's mountain-localised V500 failure, and its weaker (though still
positive-in-most-other-classes) Z500 result at the same location (Section
1.2), are a natural consequence of its patch-embedded Vision Transformer
architecture and masked-reconstruction-plus-forecasting pretraining
(Schmude et al., 2024): the model's wind/momentum pathway is already shown,
throughout this report series, to be its weakest learned balance relation
(Section 1.3 of the companion lats_FINAL and tuv_final reports), and complex
terrain is exactly where the TRUE atmospheric wind field itself departs most
strongly from the simple thermal-wind/geostrophic relation a reconstruction-
trained network would default to (orographically-forced ageostrophic flow
and mountain waves; Section 1.2 references). A model whose wind pathway is
only weakly balance-constrained even in the smooth, geostrophically
well-behaved case would be expected to fail worst exactly where the true
wind field itself is least geostrophic — which is precisely what is
observed for V500. That the SAME terrain class also shows Prithvi's weakest
(not strongest) land-class Z500 result suggests the orographic breakdown at
Land High is not confined to the explicitly wind-dependent field alone, but
partially destabilises the mass field derived alongside it too.

GraphCast's icosahedral multi-mesh GNN (Lam et al., 2023) represents the
sphere through a fixed graph hierarchy with message-passing at discrete
spatial scales; it has no terrain-following coordinate or explicit
representation of sub-grid orographic forcing. The broader NWP literature
notes that orographic gravity-wave drag parameterisation problems are
"some of the largest and most persistent circulation errors" in global NWP
and climate models specifically because of inadequate representation of
orography's dynamical effects (Section 5 references), and that fine-scale,
narrow sub-grid orography can generate non-hydrostatic effects current
parameterisations do not capture well even in physics-based models — a
purely data-driven graph model with a fixed mesh resolution would plausibly
inherit an analogous (if differently-caused) blind spot over the most
topographically complex terrain, consistent with GraphCast's observed
Land-High/Land-Mod failures here.

FCN's global spectral (AFNO) representation (Pathak et al., 2022) is
land-elevation-INSENSITIVE in its moisture failure (Section 1.4) — the
degradation appears over Ocean, the smoothest possible surface, not over
complex terrain — supporting the interpretation (Section 5, tcc report)
that FCN's moisture weakness is a field-level (spectral-smoothing) property
rather than a terrain-complexity-driven one.

Aurora's Swin-Transformer windowed-attention architecture with multi-source,
rollout-aware foundation-model training (Bodnar et al., 2024/2025) again
plausibly explains its comparatively even, terrain-organised-but-not-
terrain-broken performance: windowed attention can represent a smoothly
varying elevation-dependent response (as observed) without the sharp,
terrain-localised sign flips seen in GraphCast or Prithvi's V500.


6. FINAL SCIENTIFIC CONCLUSIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Physical understanding revealed: terrain class is a powerful diagnostic
axis precisely because it concentrates the regimes (mountains) where the
geostrophic/hydrostatic approximations that govern most of this report
series' physical interpretation are themselves weakest in the real
atmosphere — making it the most effective single breakdown, of the four in
this study, for exposing terrain-localised dynamical inconsistencies.

(2) Most dynamically consistent model: Aurora — strong, smoothly
terrain-organised gains with no sign flips anywhere in this dataset.

(3) Model benefiting most from insertion: Aurora, for Z500 specifically —
the single largest cross-variable improvement (73.5%, Land Mod) found
anywhere in this four-report series, with no terrain-localised failures
of its own; Prithvi is a solid second on Z500 over land generally (peaking
61.0% at Land Mod) but, unlike Aurora, has a genuine terrain-localised
double failure at Land High (Section 1.2).

(4) Most important variable: temperature's hydrostatic effect on Z500 is
strong over land generally for Aurora, FCN, and (with a non-monotonic
elevation profile) Prithvi; its effect on the wind field is the most
terrain-sensitive and least reliable pathway, especially over mountains,
for both Prithvi and GraphCast.

(5) Where the models fail physically: Prithvi's Z500 AND V500 both fail
specifically over Land High (its weakest land class for the former, its
only negative terrain class for the latter); GraphCast's U500/Z500 also
fail over the most topographically complex land (Land High/Land Mod) —
independent evidence, from two architecturally very different models, that
current data-driven weather models share a common difficulty with the
ageostrophic, orographically-forced component of the real atmosphere over
complex terrain, and for Prithvi this difficulty is not confined to the
wind field alone.

(6) Main takeaways for a paper/report: (a) terrain-stratified insertion
experiments can isolate dynamical failures that a flat, domain-mean or
purely zonal analysis would average away — the Prithvi Land-High and
GraphCast U500/Land-High failures are invisible in the domain-mean TUV
results but stand out clearly here; (b) the largest percentage gains
(Aurora's 73.5% Z500 over Land Mod) should be interpreted alongside the
underlying baseline error, since part of any percentage gain over complex
terrain reflects a larger starting error, not only stronger physical
coupling; and (c) orography is, in the physics-based NWP literature, a
well-known source of geostrophic-balance breakdown and some of the largest,
most persistent circulation errors in operational models — the fact that
two structurally different AI weather models independently show their
clearest dynamical failures over exactly this regime suggests the
difficulty is rooted in the real atmosphere's physics there, not merely in
any one architecture's idiosyncrasies.
