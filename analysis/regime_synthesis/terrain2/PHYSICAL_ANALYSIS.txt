0. EXPERIMENT DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Wind (U+V) is inserted (forced to ERA5 truth) and the response of T500,
Z500, and Q850 is measured across the same five terrain classes used in the
companion terrain report. This is the terrain analogue of the cloud-regime
breakdown in the companion cloud2 report.

corrected control and are valid.


1. PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION OF MODEL BEHAVIOUR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Wind constrains the mass/thermal field strongly and almost
    terrain-independently for Aurora — including, notably, at the Pole.

Aurora's T500 mature-phase improvement is 80-87% in every terrain class,
peaking at Polar (86.5%) and Land Low (85.5%); its Z500 improvement is
86-93%, again strongest over Land High (93.2%) and weakest over Ocean
(85.7%, still very strong). This near-saturated, terrain-spanning wind-to-
mass coupling reproduces, on an entirely different stratification axis, the
same near-saturation already documented for Aurora's wind insertion in the
companion cloud2 report (Section 1.1 there) and in the domain-mean TUV
report. The fact that Aurora's BEST terrain class for T500 is the Pole is
notable: high-latitude temperature is, in reality, very tightly coupled to
the wind field through thermal-wind balance precisely because the Coriolis
parameter f is largest there (the same Rossby-number argument used
throughout the companion lats_FINAL report, Section 1.2), and Aurora's
performance tracks that real physical expectation closely.

1.2 A clean model contrast: Aurora's wind-to-moisture pathway is BEST at the
    Pole; FCN's is WORST at the Pole.

Aurora's Q850 mature-phase improvement is 64.4% at the Pole — by far its
strongest moisture result, almost twice as large as its next-best terrain
class (Land High, 51.8%) and far above Ocean (31.9%) or Land Mod (27.6%).
FCN's Q850 mature-phase improvement, by contrast, is WEAKEST at the Pole
(19.7%) and strongest over Land High (50.1%) and Land Mod (47.8%). Polar
moisture amounts are small and, in reality, dominated by large-scale cold-air
advection rather than local convective/orographic moisture sources — a
regime where a purely wind-driven (advective) correction should be unusually
sufficient on its own. Aurora's result is consistent with this expectation;
FCN's is not, suggesting FCN's moisture-correction pathway depends on
information (small-scale structure, possibly orographic/convective
triggers) that is simply less available or less relevant in the polar
regime, even though wind information is, in principle, especially
sufficient there.

1.3 Prithvi's terrain-localised Z500 failure mirrors its V500 failure from
    the companion (temperature-insertion) terrain report — same location,
    same model, different inserted variable.

Prithvi's Z500 response to WIND insertion is mildly positive over Land Low
(14.0% mature), Land Mod (12.7%), and Polar (16.8%), slightly negative over
Ocean (-1.9%), and sharply NEGATIVE over Land High (-26.1% mature, despite
+8.6% at the Adjustment Phase). This is the same terrain class (Land High)
where the companion terrain report (Section 1.2 there) found Prithvi's V500
collapsing under TEMPERATURE insertion — i.e. Land High is a dynamically
fragile regime for Prithvi regardless of which single variable is inserted,
strong independent confirmation that the orographically-complex regime
itself, not the specific choice of inserted variable, is what destabilises
Prithvi's cross-variable consistency there (Section 1.2, terrain report, for
the physical mechanism: orographic forcing breaks geostrophic balance and
generates ageostrophic, gravity-wave-bearing flow that a model with a weak
or implicit balance representation is poorly equipped to handle).

1.4 Prithvi's moisture failure under wind insertion is confirmed across
    BOTH stratification axes (cloud regime and terrain), with the SAME
    single exception (the Pole).

Prithvi's Q850 mature-phase response to wind insertion is negative over
Land Low (-36.7%), Land Mod (-54.9%, the single worst value in this entire
four-report series), Ocean (-6.8%), and mildly negative over Land High
(-9.1%) — but clearly POSITIVE at the Pole (28.6%). This is the same sign
pattern documented in the companion cloud2 report (Section 1.2 there: Q850
negative in every cloud regime under wind insertion) extended here to show
it is also negative across every terrain class except one. The Pole is the
single regime, across both this report and the cloud2 report, where
Prithvi's response to wind insertion is reliably constructive for moisture —
again plausibly because polar moisture variability is dominated by
large-scale advection (the one relationship a weak but not entirely absent
wind-coupling pathway could still exploit) rather than by the convective or
small-scale processes that dominate elsewhere and that Prithvi's wind
pathway evidently cannot correct.

1.5 Prithvi's temperature field is harmed by wind insertion in every terrain
    class, mirroring its cloud-regime result (cloud2, Section 1.4) — worst
    over Land High and Ocean, least bad at the Pole.

Prithvi's T500 mature-phase response to wind insertion is negative in every
terrain class (-16.4% Land Low, -18.1% Land Mod, -34.6% Land High, -32.1%
Ocean, -6.6% Polar). Land High is, once again, Prithvi's worst terrain class
(Section 1.3's Z500 finding), but Ocean is nearly as bad (-32.1%) despite
showing only a mild Z500 degradation there (-1.9%) — indicating the
terrain-complexity explanation that accounts for Prithvi's Z500/Q850
failures over Land High does not fully account for its T500 failure over
Ocean, which may instead reflect a broader, less terrain-localised
sensitivity of Prithvi's temperature field to an artificially forced wind
state. As in cloud2 and in Section 1.4's Q850 result, the Pole is again
Prithvi's least-harmful terrain class (-6.6%, still negative but far smaller
in magnitude than any other class) — consistent with the polar
advection-dominated regime being the one setting where Prithvi's weak
wind-coupling pathway is least actively counter-productive.


2. REGIONAL AND SPATIAL ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Strongest improvements: Aurora's T500/Z500 across essentially all
terrain (Section 1.1); Aurora's Q850 and, independently, GraphCast's
Adjustment-Phase response, both peaking at the Pole.

2.2 Strongest failures: Prithvi's Z500 over Land High (Section 1.3) and
Q850 over Land Mod (Section 1.4) — the two worst results for this model in
the terrain-stratified part of this study, both landing on complex/elevated
or mid-elevation land, not at the extremes (Ocean, Polar).

2.3 Hemispheric asymmetry: as in the companion terrain report, terrain class
pools both hemispheres (Polar includes both poles; Land High includes
ranges in both hemispheres), so this dataset cannot resolve true
Northern-vs-Southern-Hemisphere asymmetry. The land-elevation and
land/ocean/polar contrasts it CAN resolve are the basis for all
interpretation here.

2.4 Smooth large-scale flow regions vs complex terrain. As in the companion
terrain report, complex terrain (Land High, Land Mod) is again where the
clearest dynamical failures concentrate (Prithvi's Z500 and Q850), while
the smoothest, most large-scale-dominated regimes (Ocean, Polar) are where
Aurora's wind-driven pathways are strongest and most reliable — the same
qualitative split found under temperature insertion, now reproduced under
wind insertion as well, reinforcing that this is a property of the
underlying terrain-complexity gradient itself rather than of either specific
insertion experiment.


3. EXPERIMENT-LEVEL INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 What wind-only insertion reveals about terrain-dependence.

For Aurora, the wind-to-mass-and-thermal pathway is strong and only mildly
terrain-dependent, with the Pole and Land High as its best classes —
consistent with both being regimes where the real atmosphere's
geostrophic/thermal-wind balance is well-defined (high f at the Pole; sharp
local height gradients over mountains). For FCN, the same pathway is
strong over land and weakest exactly at the Pole for moisture specifically
(Section 1.2) even though its mass/thermal fields do not show the same
polar weakness — a field-specific, not a uniformly terrain-driven, limit.
For Prithvi, the pathway is weak-to-moderate over most terrain and breaks
down specifically over Land High and Land Mod (Section 1.3-1.4) — the
terrain-complexity gradient is, for this model, the dominant axis of
failure, more so than the wind/momentum weakness alone would predict from
the companion cloud-regime report.

3.2 Cases where wind insertion helps one field while harming another, in
    the SAME terrain class.

Over Land High specifically, GraphCast shows T500 improving sharply in the
Adjustment Phase (65.4%) while Z500 over the same class also improves
strongly (79.7% Adjustment) — but both decay substantially by the Mature
Phase (T500 to 18.0%, Z500 to 46.6%), the terrain-class instance of the
Adjustment-to-Mature collapse documented throughout this report series for
GraphCast. Over Land Mod, the same collapse is most severe: T500 mature
turns slightly negative (-3.7%) despite a strong Adjustment-Phase value
(57.0%) — the only terrain class where GraphCast's T500 fully reverses sign.

3.3 Error propagation from the inserted variable into others, by terrain.

Propagation is reliably strong and positive for Aurora in every terrain
class for T500/Z500, and strongest specifically at the Pole for Q850
(Section 1.2). For FCN, propagation is reliably positive for T500/Z500 in
every terrain class, but weakest for Q850 specifically at the Pole — the
inverse of Aurora's pattern (Section 1.2). For Prithvi, propagation is
unreliable in sign for both Z500 and Q850, with Land High/Land Mod as the
specific terrain classes where it turns most strongly negative. For
GraphCast, propagation is strong at the Adjustment Phase everywhere but
decays toward zero or negative by the Mature Phase in most terrain classes.


4. MODEL-BY-MODEL COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AURORA: best — T500 and Z500 across essentially every terrain class
(80-93% mature); Q850 specifically at the Pole (64.4% mature, the model's
best moisture result anywhere in this four-report series). Weakest — Q850
over Land Mod (27.6% mature), still solidly positive but the model's
relative low point. Global vs local consistency: the most terrain-
consistent model in this dataset — strong and reliable everywhere, with
physically sensible peaks (Pole for moisture, Land High for mass) rather
than arbitrary ones.

FOURCASTNET: best — T500/Z500 over Land High (69.0%/81.3% mature) and Ocean
Z500 (85.5% mature, its single best Z500 value); Q850 over Land Mod/Land
High (47.8%/50.1% mature). Weakest — Q850 at the Pole (19.7% mature, the
model's clear low point and the inverse of Aurora's polar moisture
strength). Global vs local consistency: strong and terrain-consistent for
the mass/thermal fields; the one terrain-localised weak point is moisture
at the Pole specifically.

PRITHVI-WxC: best — none of the three examined fields show a reliable
positive response anywhere except the Pole: Z500 and Q850 are both
modestly-to-strongly positive specifically at the Pole (16.8% and 28.6%
mature), while T500 is negative even at the Pole, just far less severely so
(-6.6% vs -16% to -35% elsewhere) — the Pole remains the model's single most
reliable terrain class in this dataset, though for T500 that means "least
harmful" rather than "positive." Weakest — Z500 over Land High (-26.1%
mature), Q850 over Land Mod (-54.9% mature), and T500 over Land High
(-34.6% mature) — three different fields, but Land High and Ocean recur
repeatedly as Prithvi's worst terrain classes across all three variables
examined in this report. Global vs local consistency: the least
terrain-consistent model for wind-driven cross-variable propagation —
reliable (or merely least-harmful) only at the Pole, actively harmful over
the most complex or moderately complex land and, for T500 specifically, over
Ocean as well.

GRAPHCAST: best — large Adjustment-Phase gains across all terrain classes
and all three variables (54-80%). Weakest — the now-familiar Adjustment-to-
Mature collapse, most severe over Land Mod (T500 mature goes negative,
Section 3.2) and broadly present everywhere else. Global vs local
consistency: reasonable spatial/terrain organisation at short lead time;
the weakest TEMPORAL consistency of the four models, exactly as found in
the companion tcc and cloud2 reports.


5. ARCHITECTURE AND TRAINING INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Aurora's strong, terrain-spanning, physically-sensible peaks (Pole for
moisture, Land High and Pole for mass/thermal fields) are consistent with
its Swin-Transformer windowed attention combined with broad multi-source,
rollout-aware foundation-model pretraining (Bodnar et al., 2024/2025):
exposure to many regions and forecast lengths during training plausibly
yields a representation that tracks genuine physical structure (e.g. high
polar f favouring thermal-wind balance) rather than a representation
overfit to any one terrain type.

Prithvi-WxC's repeated, terrain-localised failures over Land High/Land Mod
— for V500 under temperature insertion (companion terrain report) and now
for Z500 and Q850 under wind insertion (here) — are consistent with the
same patch-embedded, masked-reconstruction-pretrained Vision Transformer
architecture discussed throughout this report series (Schmude et al.,
2024): a model whose cross-variable balance representation is comparatively
weak even over smooth terrain would be expected to fail most visibly
exactly where the real atmosphere's own balance assumptions (geostrophic,
hydrostatic) are most strongly violated by orographic forcing (Section 1.2,
1.3, and the orographic-dynamics literature cited in the companion terrain
report's References).

FCN's polar moisture weak point (Section 1.2), set against its otherwise
strong terrain-spanning mass/thermal performance, again points to a
field-specific (moisture-specific) limitation of its global spectral
(AFNO) representation (Pathak et al., 2022) rather than a terrain-specific
one — consistent with the same model's moisture weak point appearing in
every other breakdown in this report series (cloud regime, latitude band,
domain mean) regardless of which terrain or region is examined.

GraphCast's icosahedral multi-mesh GNN message-passing (Lam et al., 2023)
continues to show the same Adjustment-to-Mature collapse documented under
every experimental design in this study, here further localised to be worst
over Land Mod specifically — consistent with a processor that incorporates
inserted information well in a single pass but does not durably re-derive
or reinforce it through its own autoregressive evolution, compounded
slightly further wherever the underlying terrain dynamics are themselves
more complex.


6. FINAL SCIENTIFIC CONCLUSIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Physical understanding revealed: wind insertion's benefit is, for
Aurora, organised around genuine physical structure (high-latitude thermal-
wind balance, locally strong height gradients over mountains); for Prithvi,
it is organised around where the model's already-weak wind/momentum pathway
additionally collides with orographically-complex, ageostrophic real
dynamics (Land High, Land Mod) — the same terrain classes that broke its
V500 under the reverse (temperature) insertion in the companion report —
while its T500 field (Section 1.5) is harmed almost everywhere, including
regimes (Ocean) where its other fields are not, pointing to an additional,
less terrain-localised source of temperature sensitivity to forced wind.

(2) Most dynamically consistent model: Aurora — by a clear margin in this
dataset, with physically sensible (not arbitrary) regional peaks.

(3) Model benefiting most from insertion: FCN, in the sense of the most
uniformly positive, terrain-spanning benefit to its mass/thermal fields,
with only one clearly identified weak point (polar moisture).

(4) Most important variable: wind, for Aurora's and FCN's mass/thermal
fields across virtually all terrain; for Prithvi, wind insertion is
informative only at the Pole and is actively harmful over complex/moderate-
elevation land — the terrain breakdown sharpens, rather than changes, the
domain-mean conclusion that Prithvi's wind pathway is its weakest.

(5) Where the models fail physically: Prithvi over Land High and Land Mod
(Z500, Q850) and over Land High/Ocean for T500; GraphCast's gains decaying
by the Mature Phase essentially everywhere, most severely over Land Mod;
FCN's moisture specifically at the Pole.

(6) Main takeaways for a paper/report: (a) the SAME terrain class (Land
High) is independently identified as Prithvi's weakest regime under BOTH
the temperature-insertion (companion terrain report) and wind-insertion
(here) experiments, for different variables (V500 and Z500/Q850
respectively) — strong, design-independent evidence that orographically
complex terrain is a genuine, reproducible dynamical blind spot for this
model, not an artefact of either single experiment; (b) Aurora's
physically-sensible terrain peaks (Pole for moisture and thermal-wind
balance, mountains for mass-field gradients) are some of the most
convincing evidence in this entire four-report series that a data-driven
model can learn real, location-appropriate atmospheric physics rather than
a single global statistical rule; and (c) GraphCast's Adjustment-to-Mature
collapse is now documented across cloud regime, terrain class, and both
single-variable insertion experiments — it should be treated as a robust,
investigable property of the model's architecture or training, not as an
artefact of any one analysis.
