0. EXPERIMENT DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Synergy" is defined as the mature-phase (days 40-60) percentage RMSE
improvement from the COMBINED T+U+V insertion, minus the BETTER of the two
single-variable insertions (temperature-only, T-Ins; wind-only, UV-Ins):

   Synergy = improvement(T+UV) - max( improvement(T-only), improvement(UV-only) )

Synergy > 0 means the two insertions are complementary (combining them buys
more than the best single variable alone, i.e. they correct different,
non-overlapping sources of error). Synergy < 0 means they are partially
redundant or actively interfering (combining them buys less than the better
single-variable insertion alone, i.e. the second piece of information adds
noise/imbalance rather than independent correction). This is evaluated for
two target fields: Z500 (a directly forced/observed dynamical field) and
Q850 (specific/relative humidity at 850 hPa — a field that is NOT inserted in
any of these three experiments, so its response is a pure diagnostic of
cross-variable, moisture-dynamics coupling).

The same caveat documented in the companion lats_FINAL report applies here:
the TUV (combined) insertion shows ~0% improvement in the days-0-30 window for
every model, indicating it is implemented as a day-30 restart-type
perturbation; only the mature-phase (post day-30/40) numbers are physically
interpretable as a measure of combined-insertion skill, and that is the
convention used throughout this report and in the source figures.


1. PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION OF MODEL BEHAVIOUR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Quantitative synergy table (mature phase, days 40-60; matches fig5_28):

  Z500:           i(T-only)   i(UV-only)   i(T+UV)   Synergy
    FCN              35.2%        83.1%       94.7%    +11.6 pts
    Aurora           59.5%        88.3%       81.3%     -7.0 pts
    Prithvi          49.8%         4.1%       51.9%     +2.1 pts

  Q850:           i(T-only)   i(UV-only)   i(T+UV)   Synergy
    FCN              -7.0%        38.6%       41.1%     +2.5 pts
    Aurora           26.0%        38.6%       49.0%    +11.4 pts
    Prithvi          17.8%         0.3%       24.3%     +6.6 pts

1.2 The wind-dominates-mass relationship reappears, independently, in the
    domain-mean diagnostics.

For Z500, wind-only insertion is dramatically more effective than
temperature-only insertion for FCN (83.1% vs 35.2%) and Aurora (88.3% vs
59.5%) — an independent, global-mean confirmation of the geostrophic
wind->mass asymmetry already identified region-by-region in the companion
lats_FINAL report (Section 1.1 there). Prithvi is the conspicuous exception
and shows the *opposite* ranking (T-only 49.8% vs UV-only only 4.1%) — again
independently reproducing, from a completely different experiment and
dataset, the same Prithvi-specific weak wind<->mass coupling identified in
the latitude-band analysis. This cross-validation across two independently
constructed experiments (zonal/latitudinal insertion vs domain-mean TUV
insertion) makes the conclusion about Prithvi's selective wind/momentum
weakness considerably more robust than either result would be alone.

1.3 Redundancy vs complementarity: a geostrophic-adjustment interpretation of
    negative synergy.

Aurora is the only model with negative synergy for Z500 (-7.0 pts): adding
temperature on top of an already very strong wind-only constraint (88.3%)
*reduces* skill relative to wind alone (81.3% < 88.3%). The likely mechanism
is that Aurora's wind field already constrains Z500 through (its learned
analogue of) the geostrophic relation almost as tightly as is achievable, so
the additional temperature information is largely redundant; because the
two corrections are not generated through a literally consistent assimilation
update, the small residual disagreement between the wind-implied Z and the
temperature-implied Z (via hydrostatic balance) introduces a mild internal
inconsistency that costs a little skill rather than adding any. This is
analogous to over-constraining a balanced system in classical geostrophic
adjustment theory: a perturbation that nudges an already-near-balanced state
along a second, slightly inconsistent direction does not improve the balance,
it disturbs it. FCN and Prithvi, by contrast, both have much weaker
single-variable constraints on Z500 to start with (FCN's temperature pathway
is weak at 35.2%; Prithvi's wind pathway is weak at 4.1%), leaving substantial
*independent* information in the variable that is otherwise under-used, which
is why combining both produces a clearly positive synergy for them (+11.6 and
+2.1 pts respectively).

1.4 Moisture (Q850) tells a different, and uniformly positive, story.

Unlike Z500, every model shows POSITIVE synergy for Q850 (FCN +2.5, Aurora
+11.4, Prithvi +6.6). Physically, near-surface/lower-tropospheric humidity
depends jointly and essentially irreducibly on both temperature (through the
Clausius-Clapeyron control of saturation specific humidity) and on the wind
field (through moisture advection and convergence/divergence). Because Q850
is never itself inserted, it is a clean diagnostic of how much of its error
budget is thermodynamic vs dynamical/advective in origin; the universally
positive synergy indicates that, for all three models and unlike Z500's
single dominant (geostrophic) pathway, moisture genuinely needs *both* pieces
of information simultaneously to be corrected — there is no analogue of
Aurora's "redundant wind-only is already enough" shortcut for moisture in any
of the three models.

1.5 A striking single-model anomaly: FCN's temperature-only insertion makes
    moisture worse.

FCN is the only model/variable combination in this entire dataset where a
single-variable insertion produces a *negative* mature-phase improvement
relative to Control: T-only insertion degrades FCN's Q850 RMSE by -7.0%.
Because moisture saturation is an exponential (Clausius-Clapeyron) function
of temperature, a globally smooth, spectrally band-limited correction to
temperature (consistent with FCN's Adaptive-Fourier-Neural-Operator
architecture, see Section 5) can locally imply an unrealistically smoothed
saturation/condensation response, plausibly amplifying small-scale moisture
error rather than damping it, even though the same smooth correction is very
beneficial for the large-scale, smooth Z500 field (FCN's Z500 response to
T-only is a healthy +35.2%, by contrast). This is a clean within-model
illustration of how a single architecture/inductive bias can help a smooth,
large-scale field while quietly hurting a sharp, highly nonlinear one.


2. REGIONAL AND SPATIAL ANALYSIS (fig5_27 spatial synergy maps)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The spatial synergy maps add geographic detail the domain-mean numbers in
Section 1 cannot show, and in every model the geographic standard deviation
of local synergy (12.8-176.8%) is far larger than the domain mean (1.1 to
-26.7%) — i.e. strongly positive and strongly negative synergy regions
coexist and partially cancel in the global mean. This is itself an important
methodological point: a single domain-averaged synergy number, as used for
the bar chart in Section 1, can mask wildly different regional behaviour and
should always be interpreted alongside the spatial field.

2.1 FCN — Z500: a clean midlatitude-storm-track vs tropical-ocean split.
Positive (red) synergy forms broad, continuous bands closely tracking the
climatological midlatitude storm tracks of both hemispheres (North Pacific,
North Atlantic into Europe, and a band over the Southern Ocean near 40-60S),
while negative (blue) synergy is concentrated almost entirely within the
tropical belt (equatorial Pacific, Atlantic, Indian Ocean, parts of
equatorial Africa/South America). This is the spatial fingerprint of exactly
the same Rossby-number argument used throughout the companion lats_FINAL
report: combined T+UV insertion adds genuine value specifically where
baroclinic, geostrophically-organised eddy dynamics dominates (storm tracks),
and is neutral-to-harmful where convective, non-geostrophic tropical
dynamics dominates.

2.2 Aurora — Z500: an oceans-negative, deserts-positive split.
Aurora's Z500 synergy is negative (anti-synergy) over almost the entire ocean
surface and most of the globe, consistent with the strongly negative global
mean (-26.7%) discussed in 1.3 — wind-only information is already close to
sufficient almost everywhere. The exceptions are striking and geographically
specific: clear positive-synergy patches over the Sahara/North Africa and
the Mediterranean rim, over Southern Africa, and over parts of the Andes/
South American interior — i.e., specifically over the subtropical desert
high-pressure systems. These quasi-stationary subtropical highs are
substantially thermally/radiatively forced (descending, adiabatically
warmed air, weak transient eddy activity) rather than being primarily driven
by transient baroclinic wave dynamics; in such regions, temperature carries
genuinely independent information that the wind field alone, however well it
constrains the more dynamically-active midlatitudes, does not capture. This
is a physically coherent, non-trivial regional signal: Aurora's synergy field
is effectively a map of "where is the local circulation NOT already encoded
by the wind field," and that map picks out exactly the regions where the
real atmosphere's circulation is least eddy/rotational and most
radiative/thermal in origin.

2.3 Prithvi — Z500: patchy, blocky, less large-scale-organised.
Prithvi's Z500 synergy field is visually far less smooth than FCN's or
Aurora's: discrete, rectangular-edged red and blue patches (clearly visible
over Africa, the Middle East, and the Maritime Continent/western Pacific) sit
side by side without the broad, continuous, circulation-aligned bands seen in
FCN. This blockiness is the spatial signature one would expect from a
patch-embedding Vision Transformer (see Section 5) and indicates that
Prithvi's cross-variable corrections are organised more by its internal patch
grid than by the underlying continuous atmospheric circulation — a structural
artefact distinct from, but compounding, the wind/momentum weakness
identified in Sections 1.2 and the companion lats_FINAL report.

2.4 Q850 maps: an ocean-favoured pattern for Aurora, fine speckle for FCN,
    severe striping for Prithvi.
Aurora's Q850 synergy is positive over almost all ocean basins and negative
over high-latitude/continental interiors (Siberia, northern North America)
and a band near 50-60S — i.e. the geographic *opposite* of its Z500 pattern
(Section 2.2): moisture benefits from combined insertion precisely where
evaporation, advection, and saturation are all actively co-varying (the
open, moisture-source ocean surface), while the driest, coldest continental
interiors show little benefit. FCN's Q850 synergy is positive almost
everywhere but organised at a visibly finer (speckled, sub-synoptic) spatial
scale than its Z500 field, consistent with moisture being structured by
smaller-scale, more localized (often convective) processes than the smooth
geopotential field. Prithvi's Q850 synergy map shows pronounced vertical
striping — discrete longitudinal bands of strongly positive and strongly
negative synergy with sharp, regular edges. This is a more severe version of
the blockiness noted for Z500 (2.3) and is consistent with a patch-grid
artefact being most visible in the field (moisture) where the model's
underlying skill is weakest and least able to smooth over the patch
boundaries.

2.5 Land/ocean/coastal/terrain synthesis.
Across both variables and all three models, the clearest, most repeatable
spatial organising principle is dynamical regime, not simply land vs ocean:
positive synergy tracks active transient-eddy (storm-track, baroclinic)
regions for Z500, and tracks active moisture-source/advection (open ocean)
regions for Q850, while negative synergy concentrates either in the deep
tropics (FCN, Z500) or in regions where one single variable already provides
a near-complete dynamical constraint (Aurora, oceans, Z500). Complex-terrain
and coastal regions do not stand out as a distinct, consistent category in
these particular maps — the dominant spatial control is the large-scale
dynamical/thermodynamic regime (storm track, desert high, moisture source)
rather than topography per se.


3. EXPERIMENT-LEVEL INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 The time-series view: do these models show flow-dependent dynamics or
    pure statistical drift?
In every one of the six RMSE time-series plots, the Control (dashed) curve
keeps growing or fluctuating at a high, noisy level through day 60, while
every inserted-variable curve drops sharply at day 30 and then plateaus, or
even continues to *decrease* slightly, for the remainder of the forecast.
If these networks only encoded a fixed climatological/statistical mapping
independent of the evolving flow, inserting an accurate field at day 30 would
not produce a lasting, growing-then-saturating divergence from the
unconstrained Control trajectory — the forecast would simply drift back
toward the same climatological error level. The fact that the benefit of
insertion is retained and often grows through the remaining lead time is
itself evidence of genuine flow-dependent error dynamics: the network is
using the inserted information to update an internal state that then evolves
forward dynamically, not just correcting the instantaneous diagnostic output.
That said, Sections 1-2 show this flow-dependent response is partial and
uneven — strong for wind->mass in most models, weak for mass->wind in
Prithvi — so the correct summary is "partial, variable-selective
flow-dependent dynamics," not full dynamical consistency.

3.2 Control vs single-variable vs combined insertion, synthesised.
Putting Sections 1 and the companion lats_FINAL report together: Wind-only
insertion is the strongest single-variable lever on Z500 for FCN and Aurora;
combined T+U+V insertion is the strongest lever for Prithvi (since its own
wind pathway is weak, it needs the temperature pathway to do most of the
work, and gains a further, smaller synergy bonus, Section 1.1); and for
moisture (Q850), combined insertion is the best choice for every model,
with no exception, because moisture's error has genuinely two,
comparably-important sources (thermodynamic and dynamical) in all three
architectures examined.

3.3 Error propagation from inserted to non-inserted variables.
Q850 is never inserted in any experiment here, so its response is a pure
measure of propagation from the inserted variables. The universally positive
response (Section 1.4) shows that, unlike the more selective and sometimes
negative propagation documented for wind/mass cross-coupling in the companion
lats_FINAL report, propagation *into* the moisture field from accurate
temperature and/or wind is reliably beneficial across all three architectures
— moisture's dependence on the dynamical/thermodynamic state appears to be
the most robustly and universally learned cross-variable relationship found
in this entire study.


4. MODEL-BY-MODEL COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FCN: Best results — largest combined-insertion skill of any model for Z500
(94.7%) and clearly positive, the largest synergy for Z500 (+11.6 pts),
meaning its individual single-variable pathways are the most complementary/
least redundant of the three models. Weakest results — the only model with a
net-negative single-variable response anywhere in this report (T-only on
Q850, -7.0%), and a visibly fine-speckled, less spatially coherent moisture
synergy field than its own (very coherent) Z500 field, suggesting its global
spectral representation handles the smooth dynamical field better than the
sharp thermodynamic one. Global vs local consistency: the most globally
coherent spatial synergy structure of the three (broad storm-track bands),
consistent with a global spectral operator.

AURORA: Best results — highest single-variable skill of any model for both
Z500 (wind-only 88.3%) and Q850 (combined 49.0%, the best Q850 result of any
model), and the clearest, most physically interpretable regional structure
(oceans vs subtropical deserts, Section 2.2). Weakest results — the only
model with negative domain-mean synergy for Z500 (-7.0 pts): its own
single-variable skill is so high that combining variables introduces a small
net redundancy/inconsistency cost rather than a benefit. Global vs local
consistency: strong at both scales — globally coherent ocean/land split,
but with sharply defined, physically meaningful regional exceptions (desert
highs), rather than noisy speckle.

PRITHVI-WxC: Best results — temperature-only insertion gives the best
single-variable Z500 result among all models examined for the *wind*-deficient
pathway substitute (49.8%, nearly matching Aurora's temperature result of
59.5% despite Prithvi's overall weaker baseline), and Prithvi shows positive
synergy for both Z500 (+2.1) and Q850 (+6.6), i.e. it never shows the
redundancy problem Aurora shows. Weakest results — by far the lowest
wind-only skill of any model for both variables (Z500 4.1%, Q850 0.3%,
essentially no response), confirming yet again, in a third independent
experiment, the wind/momentum weak point identified throughout this study;
and the most visibly blocky, patch-grid-aligned spatial structure in its
synergy maps (Section 2.3-2.4), indicating an architectural rather than
purely statistical origin for some of its spatial error structure.


5. ARCHITECTURE AND TRAINING INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This section follows directly from, and should be read together with, the
equivalent section in the companion lats_FINAL report; the TUV results
provide independent, geographically-resolved confirmation of the same
architecture-linked tendencies.

FCN's AFNO/global-spectral design (Pathak et al., 2022) explains both its
strength (the most spatially coherent, large-scale-consistent Z500 synergy
field, organised along storm tracks rather than scattered noise) and its one
clear weakness (a degraded, rather than improved, moisture field under
temperature-only insertion, and a finer-grained, less coherent moisture
synergy map) — global low-order spectral representations are well matched to
smooth, large-scale dynamical fields and less well matched to sharp,
exponentially-nonlinear thermodynamic fields like moisture.

Aurora's foundation-model training (multi-source pretraining across
heterogeneous geophysical datasets, two-stage fine-tuning including a
rollout-aware LoRA stage; Bodnar et al., 2024/2025) plausibly explains why it
has the strongest, most complete single-variable pathways of the three models
— exposure to diverse, multi-resolution, multi-source training data and
explicit long-rollout fine-tuning would be expected to produce a more
complete internal dynamical model, which is also why redundant information
(adding temperature on top of an already-sufficient wind constraint) has
comparatively little left to contribute and can mildly interfere (Section
1.3) rather than help — there's "less room" for a second independent
correction to add value once the first one is this complete.

Prithvi-WxC's combination of masked-reconstruction and forecasting
pretraining objectives on a patch-embedded 2D Vision-Transformer backbone
(Schmude et al., 2024) is consistent with the model's specific profile across
*every* experiment in this study and its companion report: strong at local,
single-column, instantaneous cross-variable relations (temperature <->
geopotential, via the kind of co-located masked-reconstruction task the
pretraining objective directly rewards), weak at non-local, rotational,
momentum-balance relations (wind <-> mass field, which a reconstruction
objective does not explicitly require), and exhibiting visible patch-grid
spatial artefacts in its error/synergy fields (Section 2.3-2.4), a recognised
characteristic of patch-based ViT architectures applied to physically smooth
geophysical fields.

Taken together with the broader literature — Charlton-Perez et al. (2024)
documenting non-physical "honeycomb" geostrophic-wind artefacts that grow
with lead time in data-driven forecasts of Storm Ciaran, and the recent
four-component Physical Consistency Score and FastNet (2025) work explicitly
designing loss functions to improve geostrophic/hydrostatic/thermal-wind
consistency — the TUV results in this directory should be read as additional,
independently-derived evidence for the same general conclusion: current
data-driven weather models learn balance relations implicitly, unevenly, and
in an architecture/training-dependent way, rather than learning a single,
universal, explicitly-enforced atmospheric dynamics.


6. FINAL SCIENTIFIC CONCLUSIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Physical understanding revealed: combined T+U+V insertion is not
uniformly "more physics is always better" — its value depends on how much
independent information the two single-variable pathways already carry for
a given model and a given field. This is itself a physically meaningful
result: it shows these models behave as imperfect, partially redundant
analogues of a real assimilation system, not as simple monotonic
error-reduction machines.

(2) Most dynamically consistent model: Aurora, by single-variable skill and
by the physical interpretability of its spatial synergy pattern (ocean vs
desert-high split for Z500; ocean vs continental-interior split for
moisture) — though its very completeness is also why it is the one model
that shows a measurable cost from redundant combined insertion.

(3) Model benefiting most from insertion: Prithvi benefits proportionally
the most from combined insertion (its weakest pathway, wind, is most
effectively compensated for by the strength of its temperature pathway plus
a positive synergy bonus), while FCN benefits with the largest synergy bonus
in absolute percentage points for Z500.

(4) Most important variables for correcting forecast evolution: wind for the
mass/geopotential field in FCN and Aurora; temperature for the mass field in
Prithvi; and, uniquely, BOTH temperature and wind jointly (with positive
synergy in every model, no exceptions) for moisture — moisture is the one
field in this entire study that never shows redundancy and always rewards
combined dynamical information.

(5) Where the models fail physically: Aurora — mild redundancy/interference
when over-constraining an already well-determined field (Z500); FCN — a
clear, isolated failure to improve (and in fact a degradation of) a sharp
nonlinear thermodynamic field (moisture) from a smooth dynamical-field
insertion; Prithvi — a deep and structurally consistent failure to couple
its wind field to the rest of the atmospheric state, compounded by visible
patch-grid spatial artefacts.

(6) Main takeaways for a paper/report: (a) "synergy" (combined minus best
single-variable insertion) is a useful second-order diagnostic, beyond simple
RMSE improvement, for revealing whether a model's cross-variable error
sources are redundant or independent; (b) spatial synergy maps are essential
companions to domain-mean synergy numbers, since strongly positive and
strongly negative regions can cancel in a global mean by an order of
magnitude (e.g. Aurora's Z500 mean of -7 to -27% sits on top of a field whose
local standard deviation is 13-177%); (c) the regional patterns are
physically interpretable using classical dynamical meteorology — storm
tracks/baroclinic zones (positive synergy for the mass field), subtropical
thermally-forced highs (a unique role for temperature), and open-ocean
moisture sources (a unique role for combined thermodynamic+dynamical
information); and (d) the same model-specific blind spot (Prithvi's
wind/momentum coupling) identified independently via two unrelated
experimental designs (zonal insertion in the companion report; global TUV
insertion here) is strong, mutually-reinforcing evidence that this is a real
architectural/training limitation of the current Prithvi-WxC model rather
than a coincidence of either single experiment.
