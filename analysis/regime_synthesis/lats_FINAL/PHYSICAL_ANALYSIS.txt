0. EXPERIMENT DESIGN AND A CRITICAL METHODOLOGICAL CAVEAT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each experiment replaces (“inserts”) one dynamical field with its ERA5 truth
value at every step and asks how the RMSE of the *other*, non-inserted fields
responds, relative to a free-running Control. Three single-variable insertions
are compared:

  T-Ins     : temperature inserted   -> cross-variable response measured in U500,V500,Z500
  Wind-Ins  : U+V inserted           -> cross-variable response measured in T500,Z500
  Geo-Ins   : Z (geopotential) inserted -> cross-variable response measured in T500,U500,V500

Two complementary spatial decompositions are used:
  "latband"  – zonal bands that POOL both hemispheres symmetrically:
               Tropics (|lat|<30), Midlatitudes (30-60, both hemispheres averaged
               together), Polar (60-90, both hemispheres averaged together).
  "latslice" – four fixed Northern-Hemisphere reference latitudes (2N, 14N, 40N,
               60N), each a +/-5 deg band, representing the ITCZ edge, the
               subtropical/trade-wind belt, the core of the midlatitude jet and
               storm track, and the sub-polar/Arctic boundary, respectively.

IMPORTANT CAVEAT — read before comparing "Adjustment Phase" numbers across
models. In the underlying tables, FourCastNet and Prithvi register *exactly*
0.0% (or numerically negligible, +-0.1%) improvement for every variable and
every region during the Adjustment Phase (days 0-30), in every one of the
three experiments. This is not a physical result: it indicates that for these
two models the insertion was implemented as a restart-type perturbation that
only takes effect at/after day 30, so the "Adjustment Phase" segment is
literally identical to Control for them by construction. Aurora and
GraphCast-S, in contrast, show real, non-trivial (and sometimes negative)
Adjustment-Phase responses, consistent with insertion being applied
continuously from the initial condition for those two models. Consequently,
genuine model-to-model comparison in this dataset is only meaningful for the
MATURE PHASE (days 30-60), where all four models have been exposed to the
inserted field for an equal, non-trivial duration. All physical conclusions
below are therefore drawn primarily from the Mature-Phase numbers; Adjustment
Phase is discussed only for Aurora and GraphCast-S, where it is meaningful.

A second scope caveat: because "latband" pools the Northern and Southern
Hemisphere into one number per band, this dataset cannot, by construction,
resolve a true Northern-vs-Southern-Hemisphere asymmetry. What it *can*
resolve, and what the discussion below focuses on, is the equator-to-pole
gradient (tropics -> midlatitudes -> polar cap, and the four-point meridional
cross-section at 2N/14N/40N/60N), which is the dynamically dominant axis of
variability in the atmosphere (Rossby number and the Coriolis parameter f both
vary monotonically with latitude, not with hemisphere).


1. PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION OF MODEL BEHAVIOUR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 The central asymmetry: wind constrains mass more effectively than mass
    constrains wind.

Across every model that shows a coherent signal (Aurora, FCN, and to a lesser
extent GraphCast-S), inserting the wind field (Wind-Ins) produces a *larger and
more spatially coherent* correction of T500/Z500 than inserting temperature
(T-Ins) produces in U500/V500. Mature-phase band-mean numbers illustrate this
directly:

  Wind-Ins -> Z500   Aurora 64.9-90.0%,  FCN 71.4-83.2%   (large, coherent)
  T-Ins    -> U500   Aurora 28.7-45.5%,  FCN 15.9-22.6%   (smaller)
  T-Ins    -> V500   Aurora 18.0-27.8%,  FCN 17.7-22.5%   (smaller)

This is exactly what quasi-geostrophic theory predicts. The geostrophic
relation, f*v = (1/rho) dp/dx, lets you read the mass/pressure field's
gradient directly off the wind field at a point — it is a *local, algebraic*
diagnostic relation. Recovering wind from a mass-field anomaly is the inverse
operation: it requires differentiating the (noisy, sampled) mass field and,
in general, solving a global elliptic (Poisson-type) inversion for the
rotational wind, which is sensitive to boundary conditions and to the part of
the flow that is *not* in geostrophic balance. Inserting one accurate
pressure/height value tells a model much less about the surrounding wind than
one accurate wind vector tells it about the surrounding pressure field. The
data show that Aurora and FCN have internalised this asymmetry in a way that
is dynamically consistent with theory; GraphCast-S reproduces it only
partially (see 1.3); Prithvi does not show it at all because its wind fields
barely respond to *any* insertion (see 1.4 and Section 4).

1.2 Geostrophic/Rossby-number signature in the meridional structure.

The latslice mature-phase numbers for Wind-Ins -> T500 show a clean,
monotonic-like increase with latitude for the two best-behaved models:

  Aurora :  2N 45.3% -> 14N 62.9% -> 40N 88.1% -> 60N 87.3%
  FCN    :  2N 32.5% -> 14N 57.0% -> 40N 71.2% -> 60N 61.0%

This is the textbook Rossby-number signature of geostrophic/thermal-wind
balance: f = 2*Omega*sin(lat) is small near the equator, so the geostrophic
approximation (and with it, the thermal-wind relation linking vertical wind
shear to the horizontal temperature gradient) is formally invalid there;
errors in the wind field are not tightly tied to errors in temperature at low
latitude. Approaching 40-60N — the climatological core of the midlatitude jet
and baroclinic storm tracks — f is large, thermal-wind balance is tight, and
an accurate wind field very effectively pins down the temperature field. The
fact that this latitude dependence emerges *purely from a perturbation
experiment*, without it being imposed, is itself evidence that Aurora and FCN
have learned a flow-dependent, latitude-dependent dynamical relationship
rather than a single global statistical regression between variables.

1.3 Where the “well-behaved” models break: equatorial wind insertion.

GraphCast-S is the most informative failure case. Under Wind-Ins, its T500
response at 2N in the mature phase is -50.2% (a *doubling-or-worse* of error
relative to Control), while its 40N response is +58.9%. The same model that
benefits enormously from wind information at the jet-stream latitudes is
actively damaged by the same kind of information at the equator. The
mechanistic reading is that GraphCast-S has learned an internally consistent,
but specifically extratropical, geostrophic-style coupling between wind and
mass fields. Forcing the equatorial wind field to its (non-geostrophically
organised, often divergent, convectively coupled) true value injects
information that is incompatible with the balance relationship the network
relies on, and the network has no mechanism to safely "ignore" or
down-weight that information the way it apparently can poleward of ~15N.
This is a direct illustration of geostrophic-adjustment failure: a
locally-forced perturbation that is out of balance with the rest of the
modelled state should, in a true dynamical system, radiate away as
inertia-gravity waves and leave the slow, balanced flow largely unaffected
(the classical Rossby/Charney adjustment problem). A network that has not
learned this adjustment mechanism instead lets the imbalance contaminate the
forecast.

1.4 Prithvi: strong thermodynamic/hydrostatic coupling, weak dynamical
    (wind) coupling.

Prithvi shows a strikingly consistent asymmetry across *all three*
experiments: its Z500 and T500 fields respond well to insertion of other
variables (Geo-Ins -> T500: 34-48% mature; T-Ins -> Z500: 59-69% mature, the
best of any model at the pole), but its U500/V500 fields are flat-to-negative
under every experiment (T-Ins -> U500: -2.3 to +2.8%; Geo-Ins -> U500: -2.5 to
+1.0%; Wind-Ins, where U/V is the inserted variable itself, still leaves
T500 *negative*, i.e. -22.1% to -4.5%, across the board). Two physically
distinct balance relations are being probed here: the hydrostatic/hypsometric
relation that ties temperature to the vertical structure of geopotential
(a local, vertical, single-column relation), and the geostrophic/gradient-wind
relation that ties the wind to the horizontal structure of the mass field
(a non-local, horizontal relation involving Coriolis and pressure-gradient
forces). The data indicate Prithvi has learned the former far better than the
latter. Even when wind is *directly inserted* as the controlled experimental
variable, Prithvi's temperature field gets worse, not better — implying the
insertion creates a state that is internally inconsistent with whatever
implicit relationship Prithvi's decoder uses to update temperature, and the
model effectively propagates an imbalance, rather than damping it via
something resembling geostrophic adjustment.

1.5 Statistical correlation vs flow-dependent dynamics — a summary verdict.

The fact that improvement magnitudes change systematically with latitude
band, and do so in a way that tracks known Rossby-number/baroclinicity
scaling (1.2), is strong evidence that Aurora and FCN are not merely encoding
a fixed climatological covariance between variables; the response is
flow/region dependent in a dynamically sensible way. GraphCast-S shows the
same large-scale tendency but with a localized, equatorial pathology (1.3),
indicating a dynamically-aware but imperfectly generalised representation.
Prithvi's selective failure — good thermodynamic coupling, poor dynamical
(wind) coupling, in every experiment — is most consistent with a model that
has learned strong *single-column / vertical* statistical-physical structure
(temperature-geopotential covariance) but a much weaker *horizontal,
rotational* dynamical constraint. None of the four models show evidence of
having learned a fully closed dynamical balance (in which inserting any one
of T, Z, or (U,V) would re-balance all the others to a comparable degree);
each has a distinct, identifiable blind spot.


2. REGIONAL AND SPATIAL ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Tropics vs midlatitudes vs polar cap (latband).

 - T-Ins -> Z500 (mature): Aurora is high and fairly flat (53.8-62.3% across
   all three bands) — consistent with hydrostatic T->Z coupling being a local
   vertical relation with little latitude dependence. GraphCast-S, by
   contrast, is strongly tropics-favoured (40.0% Tropics vs -1.0%
   Midlatitudes vs +2.1% Polar) — i.e. for GraphCast-S the T->Z pathway is
   only working where deep convection and moist thermodynamics dominate the
   tropical mass field, and is essentially neutral-to-broken in the
   extratropics, the opposite regional dependence from its Wind-Ins result
   (1.3). The two experiments together paint GraphCast-S as a model whose
   tropical and extratropical dynamics are represented by largely separate,
   imperfectly reconciled internal mechanisms.

 - Wind-Ins -> T500/Z500 (mature): every model that responds at all
   (Aurora, FCN, GraphCast-S) improves more in the Midlatitude/Polar bands
   than in the Tropics (e.g. FCN: T500 57.0% Tropics -> 70.1% Midlat -> 59.9%
   Polar; Aurora: 54.6% -> 84.3% -> 85.9%). This is the band-pooled version of
   the meridional jet-core signature already discussed in 1.2.

 - Geo-Ins is the most spatially *uniform* and best-behaved experiment for
   every model, including GraphCast-S (T500: 65.5%, 68.9%, 61.9% across
   Tropics/Midlat/Polar — no sign flips anywhere). Physically, geopotential
   height is the field most directly tied to *both* the thermal structure
   (hydrostatically) and the wind field (geostrophically) at essentially all
   latitudes outside the deep tropics, and it remains a meaningful,
   well-defined quantity even near the equator (unlike "the equatorial
   geostrophic wind", which is ill-defined as f -> 0). Z is, in effect, the
   closest thing in this dataset to a master/streamfunction-like variable,
   and inserting it gives the most dynamically-consistent, regionally
   uniform correction of any single-variable experiment.

2.2 Meridional cross-section (latslice: 2N / 14N / 40N / 60N).

The four reference latitudes sample, in order, the ITCZ edge, the subtropical
trade-wind/subsidence belt, the core of the midlatitude jet/storm track, and
the sub-polar/Arctic boundary. Read together with the band results, the
slice data confirm that the *transition* in model skill is centred near
20-30N — i.e. near the climatological edge of the Hadley cell / subtropical
jet — rather than being smooth across the whole meridional domain. GraphCast-S
in particular shows a near-discontinuous change in sign between 14N and 40N
in both the Wind-Ins and T-Ins slice results, placing its dynamical "blind
spot" specifically in the tropical/subtropical transition zone rather than
diffusely across low latitudes.

2.3 Land, ocean, coastal and complex-terrain dependence.

The latband/latslice decomposition used in these specific figures is purely
zonal (latitude-only) and does not stratify by land/ocean/coastline, so this
particular dataset cannot directly support land-vs-ocean or terrain-complexity
claims; that distinction is addressed instead in the companion analysis of
the TUV spatial synergy maps (see common/tuv_final/PHYSICAL_ANALYSIS.txt),
which does carry full 2-D geographic information and shows, e.g., a clear
land/desert vs ocean split in Aurora's response and a midlatitude-storm-track
vs tropical-ocean split in FCN's response. The latitude-band results in this
directory should be read as the *zonally-averaged summary* of that more
detailed spatial picture: the band/slice latitude dependence documented above
is consistent with, and is partly explained by, the regional patterns
detailed in the TUV synergy maps.


3. EXPERIMENT-LEVEL INTERPRETATION (Control vs T-Ins vs Wind-Ins vs Geo-Ins)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 What each insertion reveals about dependence structure.

 - T-Ins isolates how much of a model's error in the *rotational/wind* and
   *mass* fields is attributable to temperature/thermodynamic error. The
   weak U/V response (1.1) shows that, for every model, temperature error is
   NOT the dominant source of wind-field error — wind error has its own,
   largely independent error growth pathway (most likely the directly
   modelled momentum/vorticity dynamics).

 - Wind-Ins isolates how much of the mass/thermal-field error is attributable
   to wind error, and the answer (for Aurora, FCN, partially GraphCast-S) is
   "a great deal, especially at jet-stream latitudes" — consistent with the
   physical fact that, outside the tropics, synoptic-scale mass-field
   evolution is very largely a *consequence* of the rotational wind field
   (vorticity advection, baroclinic development) rather than an independent
   error source.

 - Geo-Ins isolates the centrality of the geopotential/streamfunction field
   itself. That it produces the most uniform, largest, and most consistently
   positive cross-variable correction of the three experiments, for three of
   the four models, supports the classical view (going back to Charney's
   quasi-geostrophic formulation, in which the geopotential/streamfunction is
   the single prognostic field from which both temperature, via the
   hypsometric relation, and wind, via the geostrophic relation, can be
   diagnosed) that Z is the most informationally "central" of the variables
   examined here for these models, much as it is in the real atmosphere's
   quasi-geostrophic dynamics.

3.2 Does combined T+U+V insertion behave more consistently than single-
    variable insertion?

This specific figure set only contains single-variable insertions (T, Wind,
or Z alone); the joint T+U+V ("TUV") insertion is analysed quantitatively in
the companion TUV report, where it is shown that combined insertion is *not*
uniformly better than the best single-variable insertion — for Aurora's
Z500, combining T with already-sufficient wind information measurably
*hurts* relative to wind alone (negative synergy), whereas for FCN and
Prithvi the two pieces of information are complementary (positive synergy).
The single-variable results here explain why: Aurora's wind-only correction
of Z500 is already very large (87-90% in the mature phase, Section 1.1),
leaving little room for temperature to add independent value and some risk
that an imperfect temperature insertion reintroduces a small inconsistency.
FCN's and Prithvi's single-variable corrections are smaller and more
asymmetric (FCN: 71-83% from wind, weaker from temperature in the band
results; Prithvi: strong from temperature, weak from wind), so the two
sources of information are still largely independent/complementary for them.

3.3 Cases where one field improves while another is harmed.

Wind-Ins is the clearest example: for GraphCast-S, inserting U/V improves
Z500 in the midlatitudes/poles (up to +69.9% at the slice level) while
simultaneously *degrading* T500/Z500 at the equator (down to -50.2%). The
likely dynamical reason is discussed in 1.3 — the equatorial wind field
carries information (divergence, convective coupling) that is not organised
by the same balance the model uses poleward, so enforcing it there propagates
error rather than correcting it. A second example is Prithvi under Wind-Ins,
where the directly-inserted variable (wind) is, by construction, perfect, yet
the diagnostically-coupled variable (temperature) gets *worse* nearly
everywhere — direct evidence that errors can propagate from an artificially
"corrected" variable into an uncorrected one when the model's internal
cross-variable consistency is weak.

3.4 Error propagation from inserted to non-inserted variables.

The asymmetric and sometimes-negative responses documented throughout
Sections 1-3 show that error propagation in these networks is not simply
"more truth in -> less error out" for every field. A perfectly-specified
input variable can still degrade other fields if the network's internal
representation does not enforce the corresponding balance relation; in such
cases the insertion is effectively an out-of-distribution forcing rather than
a beneficial constraint, and the network has no mechanism analogous to
geostrophic/inertia-gravity-wave adjustment to dissipate the resulting
imbalance.


4. MODEL-BY-MODEL COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AURORA
  Best variables/regions: Z500 and T500 respond very strongly to almost any
  insertion, and the response *grows* with latitude in a way that matches
  thermal-wind/geostrophic theory (Section 1.2). Aurora is the only model
  that shows a clear, real Adjustment-Phase response (i.e. insertion-from-IC
  protocol), letting it demonstrate dynamically sensible behaviour even
  before day 30.
  Weaker regions: U/V recovery from T-Ins alone is comparatively modest
  (Aurora's smallest cross-variable response of the three experiments),
  consistent with the general "wind->mass easy, mass->wind hard" asymmetry
  (1.1), though Aurora still shows the best wind recovery of the four models.
  Local vs global consistency: Aurora shows the most *globally* coherent,
  latitude-organised response of the four models — its improvements track
  large-scale dynamical regimes (Rossby number, jet latitude) rather than
  showing the regionally fragmented behaviour seen in GraphCast-S or Prithvi.
  Lead-time behaviour: Aurora's mature-phase responses are uniformly larger
  than its adjustment-phase responses, indicating that the benefit of
  insertion compounds over the forecast window rather than being a one-time
  initial correction — i.e. an accurate constraint early in the rollout
  continues to pay off dynamically as the forecast evolves.

FOURCASTNET (FCN)
  Best variables/regions: a consistent, monotonic-ish increase in skill
  from Tropics to Midlatitudes under Wind-Ins (Section 2.1), and the largest,
  most uniform cross-variable gains of any model under Geo-Ins (T500: 56-83%
  across all bands and slices). FCN never produces a *negative* mature-phase
  cross-variable response anywhere in this dataset — its weakest results are
  small-positive, not pathological.
  Weaker regions: FCN's Adjustment-Phase response is, by the protocol
  described in Section 0, identically zero, so nothing can be said about its
  early-forecast dynamical behaviour from this dataset; only its mature-phase
  (already-equilibrated) behaviour is assessed here.
  Local vs global consistency: FCN's smooth, monotonic latitude dependence
  and complete absence of sign flips is the signature of a *globally*
  smooth/consistent response, plausibly related to its global, spectral
  (Fourier-domain) internal representation (see Section 5).

GRAPHCAST-S
  Best variables/regions: extratropical T500/Z500 response to Wind-Ins is
  excellent (up to 93.0% Z500 at 40N, mature phase) — among the strongest
  results of any model in this entire dataset.
  Weaker regions: a severe, repeated equatorial pathology — large *negative*
  responses at 2N/14N under Wind-Ins (T500: -50.2% at 2N) and at high
  latitude under T-Ins (Z500: -7.5% at 40N, -12.1% at 60N). GraphCast-S is
  the only model in this dataset whose cross-variable response changes sign
  with latitude within a single experiment, in both directions, depending on
  which experiment.
  Local vs global consistency: GraphCast-S looks like two different models
  spliced together along a latitude axis — strongly globally consistent in
  the extratropics, locally inconsistent/noisy near the equator. This is the
  weakest *generalisation* of the four models, even though its
  best-case extratropical skill rivals Aurora's.

PRITHVI-WxC
  Best variables/regions: T500 and Z500 respond very well to insertion of
  any *other* thermodynamic/mass variable (T-Ins -> Z500 reaches the highest
  mature-phase Polar-band value of any model, 68.9%; Geo-Ins -> T500 is
  positive at every latitude, 34-50%).
  Weaker regions: U500/V500 are essentially unresponsive or mildly *negative*
  under every experiment, including when wind is the variable directly
  inserted (Wind-Ins -> T500 is negative everywhere, -22.1% to -4.5%). This
  is the single most consistent and most physically significant weak point
  identified anywhere in this report: Prithvi shows a persistent inability to
  couple its momentum (wind) representation to its mass/thermodynamic fields
  in either direction.
  Local vs global consistency: Prithvi's deficiency is consistent across
  experiments (suggesting a structural, architecture/training-level cause —
  see Section 5) rather than regionally patchy the way GraphCast-S's is.


5. ARCHITECTURE AND TRAINING INTERPRETATION (FCN, Aurora, Prithvi)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FourCastNet (Pathak et al., 2022, arXiv:2202.11214) is built on Adaptive
Fourier Neural Operators (AFNO): each layer performs a global Fourier
transform, a learned filtering of the (low-order) spectral coefficients, and
an inverse transform. Every spatial point therefore has, in principle, a
global receptive field from the very first layer, and the representation is
explicitly organised by spatial wavenumber. This is a plausible structural
explanation for two observations above: (i) FCN's complete absence of sign
flips and its smooth, monotonic latitude dependence (a global, low-order
spectral operator cannot easily represent a sharp, localized inconsistency at
one latitude band while behaving well elsewhere — its inductive bias favours
broad, large-scale consistency over local "patchwork" behaviour); and (ii) in
the companion TUV analysis, FCN's only outright *failure* case (Q850
degrading under T-only insertion) again involves a sharp, highly non-linear,
small-scale field (moisture, governed by the exponential Clausius-Clapeyron
relation) — exactly the kind of field a low-order spectral truncation would
be expected to represent and correct less precisely than a smooth, large-scale
field like Z500.

Aurora (Bodnar et al., 2024/2025, Nature; arXiv:2405.13063) uses a 3D Swin
Transformer processor with 3D-Perceiver encoders/decoders, pretrained as a
genuine foundation model across multiple heterogeneous geophysical datasets
(reanalysis, forecasts, climate projections) and then fine-tuned in two
stages, including a rollout/LoRA fine-tuning stage explicitly aimed at
multi-step, long-lead-time forecast stability. Swin attention is windowed but
hierarchically merged across scales, giving Aurora both a degree of locality
(useful for representing latitude-dependent regimes without forcing one global
rule on the whole sphere) and, through window-shifting/hierarchy, an effective
large-scale receptive field. This architectural combination is a plausible
explanation for why Aurora is the model that most cleanly reproduces the
*latitude-dependent* (Rossby-number-like) structure of geostrophic/thermal-
wind balance documented in Section 1.2 — its inductive bias is flexible
enough to represent different effective dynamical regimes at different
latitudes, unlike FCN's globally uniform spectral operator, while its
multi-stage, multi-dataset, rollout-aware fine-tuning plausibly explains why
it is the model that benefits most, and earliest (non-zero Adjustment Phase),
from accurate constraints.

Prithvi-WxC (Schmude et al., 2024, arXiv:2409.13598) is a scalable 2D Vision
Transformer, pretrained on MERRA-2 with a *combination* of masked
reconstruction and forecasting objectives, and built around patch embeddings
with local+global attention. Two architectural/training features map
directly onto the model's observed weak point: (1) the masked-autoencoder-style
reconstruction objective trains the network to fill in *missing values of a
single field from its own spatial neighbourhood and from co-located other
fields at the same instant*, which is naturally well-suited to learning local,
instantaneous, single-column relations such as the hydrostatic/hypsometric
T-Z coupling, but does not explicitly require the network to learn the
*non-local, rotational* mass-wind relation that real geostrophic balance
demands; and (2) patch embeddings with a finite patch size impose a
block/tile structure on the internal representation, which is consistent with
the visibly blocky, patch-aligned spatial artefacts seen in Prithvi's spatial
synergy maps (companion TUV report) — a known signature of patch-based ViT
architectures applied to physically smooth fields. Together these are a
coherent explanation for why Prithvi learns thermodynamic/hydrostatic
consistency well but momentum/geostrophic consistency poorly: the training
objective and patch-local attention structure simply provide much weaker
pressure to learn the latter.

Local vs global attention, more generally: windowed/local attention
(Prithvi's patches, Aurora's Swin windows) is well suited to representing
regionally-varying dynamical regimes (e.g. the tropics-to-jet transition) but,
without sufficiently effective long-range mixing, risks under-representing
genuinely global phenomena — planetary waves, teleconnections, and the
elliptic, whole-sphere character of the wind-from-mass inversion discussed in
1.1. Global spectral mixing (FCN's AFNO) is the opposite trade-off: excellent
at enforcing large-scale, planetary-wave-consistent behaviour, but with a
coarser effective resolution of localized regime transitions and small-scale,
sharp fields. GraphCast (Lam et al., 2023, Science; not analysed
architecturally in depth here per the scope of this study, but present in
this dataset as GraphCast-S) sits structurally between these extremes — a
multi-mesh graph neural network with explicit multi-scale message passing on
an icosahedral mesh — and its observed behaviour (excellent multi-scale,
extratropical consistency; a sharp equatorial failure mode) is broadly
consistent with a mesh/message-passing architecture that resolves
extratropical baroclinic scales well but is more exposed to the
small-Rossby-number, convectively organised tropical regime, where its
graph-based long-range mixing may not adequately separate balanced
(rotational) from unbalanced (divergent) information.

In summary, none of the three models examined in architectural depth appear
to learn the underlying balance physics *explicitly* (none have an explicit
geostrophic, hydrostatic, or thermal-wind constraint built into their loss or
architecture in this dataset's experiments); all three show behaviour that is
*statistically learned but with genuine, regionally-organised dynamical
structure superimposed* — i.e. implicit, partial physical learning, with the
specific balance relation that is well captured differing systematically by
architecture and training objective. This view is independently corroborated
in the published literature: Charlton-Perez et al. (2024, npj Climate and
Atmospheric Science) document literal "honeycomb" non-physical artefacts in
the *geostrophic wind* diagnosed from several data-driven models during Storm
Ciarán, growing with lead time, and a 2025 four-component "Physical
Consistency Score" framework (covering geostrophic balance, non-divergence,
thermal-wind balance, and hydrostatic consistency) has been proposed
specifically because standard RMSE metrics do not reveal this kind of
selective, balance-relation-specific failure — exactly the kind of failure
this insertion-experiment dataset has independently uncovered for Prithvi's
wind field and GraphCast-S's equatorial response.


6. FINAL SCIENTIFIC CONCLUSIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) What do these experiments reveal about the physical understanding of each
model? All four models show evidence of *some* learned dynamical structure —
none behave as if cross-variable relationships were absent — but each has a
distinct, reproducible blind spot: GraphCast-S at the equator/subtropical
transition; Prithvi in the wind/momentum field specifically; FCN in
sharp/small-scale, non-spectrally-smooth fields; Aurora shows the fewest
blind spots in this dataset, but even Aurora's gains saturate or mildly
reverse when redundant information is added (see companion TUV report,
Section 3.2 here).

(2) Which model appears most dynamically consistent? Aurora, by the criteria
available in this dataset: the largest, most latitude-coherent, most
theory-consistent (Rossby-number-scaled) response, the fewest sign flips, and
the only model exhibiting a real (non-zero) Adjustment-Phase response,
indicating its dynamical sensitivity operates from the very start of the
forecast rather than only after long equilibration.

(3) Which model benefits most from insertion? In relative terms, Prithvi
shows the largest *relative* jump in skill for the variables it is capable of
correcting (T500/Z500), because its uncorrected baseline for those fields is
weaker to begin with; Aurora shows the largest *absolute* and most *broadly
distributed* benefit across variables and regions.

(4) Which variables seem most important for correcting forecast evolution?
Wind (U,V) is the single most informative insertion for correcting the
mass/thermal field in the extratropics (Section 1.1-1.2); geopotential height
is the most informative and most uniformly beneficial insertion across all
latitudes and all models (Section 2.1, 3.1), consistent with its role as a
near-streamfunction, dynamically central variable in quasi-geostrophic theory.
Temperature alone is the least effective single-variable insertion for
correcting the rotational wind field, for every model.

(5) Where do the models fail physically? At the equator/low-Rossby-number
regime (GraphCast-S, sharply); in the rotational-wind <-> mass-field coupling
specifically (Prithvi, persistently, regardless of which variable is
inserted); and, more subtly, in redundant-information regimes where a second,
already-largely-redundant accurate constraint can be mildly destabilising
rather than neutral (Aurora's Z500 under combined insertion, detailed in the
TUV companion report).

(6) Main takeaways for a paper/report: (a) single-variable OSSE-style
insertion experiments are an effective, inexpensive diagnostic for exposing
*which* balance relation (hydrostatic, geostrophic/thermal-wind, or neither)
an AI weather model has implicitly learned, without needing to instrument the
model internally; (b) the diagnostic results are reproducible across two
independent latitude decompositions (band and slice) and are physically
interpretable in terms of classical dynamical meteorology (Charney's
quasi-geostrophic theory, the Rossby-number scaling of geostrophic balance,
and the classical geostrophic/inertia-gravity-wave adjustment problem); (c)
architecture and training objective leave a clear, identifiable fingerprint
on *which* balance relation is well learned (global spectral operators ->
smooth, large-scale consistency; foundation-model multi-scale attention with
rollout fine-tuning -> the most latitude-coherent geostrophic-like response;
masked-reconstruction-pretrained patch-ViT -> strong local/vertical
thermodynamic consistency but weak rotational/wind consistency); and (d) this
is fully consistent with, and adds an OSSE-style, cross-variable causal
dimension to, the wider literature documenting that current data-driven
weather models satisfy classical balance relations only partially and
unevenly (Charlton-Perez et al. 2024; FastNet, 2025; the four-component
Physical Consistency Score literature).
