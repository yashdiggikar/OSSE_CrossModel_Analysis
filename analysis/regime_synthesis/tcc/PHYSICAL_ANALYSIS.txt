0. EXPERIMENT DESIGN AND DATA CAVEATS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Temperature is inserted (forced to ERA5 truth) and the response of three
wind/mass cross-variables (U500, V500, Z500) plus 700 hPa moisture (Q700;
specific humidity for Aurora/Prithvi/GraphCast, relative humidity % for FCN)
is measured, stratified into four ERA5 total-cloud-cover (TCC) regimes:
  Clear   TCC < 10%       (radiatively/dynamically quiescent, subsidence-prone)
  Cirrus  10% <= TCC < 40% (thin/high cloud, weak forcing)
  Mixed   40% <= TCC < 70% (transitional, often frontal/baroclinic)
  Opaque  TCC >= 70%       (deep, organised cloud — frontal/convective systems)
Two phases are compared: Adjustment (day 30-40) and Mature (day 40-60).

As in the companion latitude-band report (common/lats_FINAL/PHYSICAL_ANALYSIS.txt)
and the TUV report (common/tuv_final/PHYSICAL_ANALYSIS.txt), Aurora and
GraphCast show real, non-zero Adjustment-Phase responses (insertion from the
initial condition), while this dataset's FCN and Prithvi numbers are also
non-trivial in the Adjustment Phase here (unlike the zonal lats_FINAL
dataset) — the cloud-regime pipeline applies the same single 60-day run to
all models and reads out the existing trajectories, so all four models'
Adjustment-Phase numbers are meaningful here. Mature-phase numbers remain the
primary basis for cross-model comparison since they reflect a longer,
better-equilibrated exposure to the inserted field.


1. PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION OF MODEL BEHAVIOUR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 A cloud regime is, physically, a proxy for the dominant local dynamical
    regime — not just a radiative label.

Clear-sky columns are typically associated with subsidence (descending,
adiabatically warming/drying air under high pressure — subtropical highs,
post-frontal cold-air advection); Opaque columns are typically associated
with active ascent — frontal overrunning, baroclinic lifting, organised
convection. Mixed and Cirrus regimes sit on the transition. Because of this,
a model's skill profile across cloud regimes is, in effect, a second,
independent probe of the same baroclinic-activity axis already identified
in the companion latitude-band report (Section 1.2 there), obtained from
instantaneous local cloud state rather than from zonal latitude bands.
This is consistent with the literature finding that cloud cover is not just
a passive label but actively conditions forecast-error statistics — Tmax/Tmin
temperature-forecast errors are measurably larger when cloud cover is below
~40% than when skies are overcast (i.e. error characteristics genuinely
differ by regime), and clouds are recognised as "a major source of forecast
error" precisely because they index the predictability of moist atmospheric
flow (Frontiers in Earth Science, 2023; cited in Section 5/References).

1.2 Aurora and FCN: skill that tracks the regime's dynamical activity, not
    just its cloud amount.

FCN's Z500 mature-phase improvement from T-insertion is lowest in Clear
(21.0%), rises through Cirrus (32.1%) and peaks in Mixed (37.6%), then falls
back in Opaque (28.1%) — an inverted-U with its peak in the regime most
associated with active frontal/baroclinic lifting (Mixed), not in the most
heavily clouded regime. The same inverted-U, peaking at Mixed, appears in
FCN's U500 (18.1% -> 20.8% -> 25.0% -> 19.5%). This is the cloud-regime
analogue of the storm-track-aligned positive-synergy bands found in the
companion TUV spatial maps (Section 2.1 there): temperature information is
most dynamically informative precisely where baroclinic/frontal activity (and
hence the thermal-wind coupling between temperature and the rotational flow)
is most active, and somewhat less informative in either the most quiescent
(Clear) or the most saturated/convectively-dominated (Opaque) regimes, where
non-baroclinic processes (radiative cooling, latent-heat release) compete
with or dominate the baroclinic signal.

Aurora's profile is flatter and uniformly strong (Z500 mature: 65.5%, 62.7%,
59.9%, 55.9% — a mild monotonic decline from Clear to Opaque, rather than
FCN's inverted-U), indicating Aurora's hydrostatic temperature-to-geopotential
coupling (Section 1.4 of the companion lats_FINAL report) is robust across
the whole dynamical spectrum, with only a modest erosion in the most actively
clouded/convective regime, where latent-heat release and condensation
introduce mass-field structure that is not simply diagnosable from the dry
hydrostatic relation alone.

1.3 Prithvi: a solid, cloud-regime-independent Z500 pathway (second only to
    Aurora's, not the strongest), and a moisture pathway that runs in the
    OPPOSITE regime direction from Aurora's.

Prithvi's Z500 improvement from T-insertion is large and nearly flat across
all four regimes (49.2% Clear, 48.5% Cirrus, 51.6% Mixed, 51.9% Opaque — a
narrow ~3-point spread, the flattest Z500 profile of any model in this
report series). This reconfirms, independently, the hydrostatic/thermodynamic
coupling already identified for Prithvi in the companion lats_FINAL and
tuv_final reports, and shows that pathway is essentially insensitive to the
local cloud/dynamical regime — though its magnitude here is second to
Aurora's (55.9-65.5% mature across the same four regimes, Section 1.2), not
the largest of the four models as an earlier version of this report stated
(see Section 4 for the corrected model ranking).

Prithvi's moisture (Q700) pathway tells the opposite regional story from
Aurora's: Aurora's Q700 improvement is highest in Clear (21.4%) and lowest in
Cirrus (9.4%); Prithvi's Q700 improvement is lowest (and actually negative)
in Clear (-11.8%), crosses to near-zero in Cirrus (-0.7%), and turns positive
through Mixed (8.2%) to Opaque (14.0%) — the same monotonic rise toward the
most heavily clouded regime as before, just starting from a negative
baseline in the two clearest regimes rather than a small positive one.
Physically, this means Aurora's temperature-to-moisture coupling is most
useful in radiatively-controlled, subsidence-dominated (Clear) conditions,
where the moisture field is closely tied to the (well-captured) thermal/
hydrostatic state, while Prithvi's temperature-to-moisture coupling is not
just weaker but actively harmful in those same Clear/Cirrus conditions, and
only becomes net-useful in actively clouded conditions, where condensation
and latent-heat release create the strongest local temperature-moisture
covariance for its reconstruction-style training objective (Section 5) to
exploit. Two architecturally different models therefore extract the same
kind of cross-variable information from opposite ends of the cloud-regime
spectrum — a genuinely informative contrast that would be invisible without
this regime-resolved breakdown — but Prithvi's version of that contrast is
now known to dip negative at one end, not just weaker.

1.3b Prithvi's wind response (U500, V500) is modest-to-mixed across every
     regime, including outright negative for U500 in Clear.

Prithvi's U500 mature-phase improvement is small and non-monotonic across
regimes (-4.8% Clear, 5.9% Cirrus, 9.3% Mixed, 3.2% Opaque) — the only model
in this dataset whose wind response goes negative in any regime. V500 is
more reliably positive but still modest (13.5% Clear, 12.8% Cirrus, 9.1%
Mixed, 6.3% Opaque). Both wind components are well below Aurora's (U500
40.9-44.6%, V500 20.4-27.5%) or even FCN's (U500 18.1-25.0%) in every
regime, continuing the wind/momentum weak point documented independently in
the companion lats_FINAL and tuv_final reports.

1.4 GraphCast: a temporal sign reversal, not a regional one, in this dataset.

In the zonal lats_FINAL dataset, GraphCast-S's main pathology was a sharp
*regional* (equatorial) sign flip. In this cloud-regime dataset, GraphCast's
main pathology is a *temporal* sign flip: its Z500 Adjustment-Phase response
to T-insertion is positive in every regime (25.9%, 8.2%, 11.9%, 14.1%), but
by the Mature Phase it has gone negative in three of the four regimes
(-4.4% Clear, -8.5% Cirrus, -3.5% Mixed, only +1.5% Opaque). The insertion
helps GraphCast's geopotential field in the short term and then actively
degrades it after continued exposure, regardless of which cloud regime is
examined. This is consistent with — and reinforced by — the same pattern
found independently in the companion cloud2 (UV-insertion) and terrain2
analyses (see their Section 1 and Section 4): GraphCast's short-term gains
from insertion are not dynamically "locked in" by its own forward evolution,
and tend to decay or reverse the longer the forecast continues.

1.5 FCN's moisture failure is now confirmed a THIRD independent way.

FCN's Q700 mature-phase "improvement" from T-insertion is negative in every
single cloud regime (-6.0% Clear, -7.7% Cirrus, -0.01% Mixed, -2.7% Opaque) —
the same sign of failure already identified for FCN's moisture field in the
companion TUV report (Section 1.5 there: T-only insertion degraded FCN's
Q850 by -7.0% in the domain mean) and now reproduced here across every cloud
regime in an entirely different, independently-constructed dataset (700 hPa
RH-conditioned-on-cloud-regime rather than domain-mean Q850). Three
independent analyses (TUV domain-mean, this cloud-regime breakdown, and the
terrain breakdown below) all show the same result: FCN's smooth, globally
spectral temperature correction does not translate into improved moisture,
and frequently makes it slightly worse, regardless of region, terrain, or
cloud state. This elevates the finding from "FCN's moisture is a weak point"
to "FCN's moisture is essentially un-helped, and often mildly harmed, by
temperature information specifically" — a structural, not incidental,
property of this model under this experiment.


2. REGIONAL AND SPATIAL ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This dataset stratifies by instantaneous local cloud state rather than by
fixed geography, so it cannot directly answer "which continent/ocean basin"
questions (those are addressed in the companion TUV spatial-synergy-map
report and, for fixed terrain classes, in the terrain/terrain2 reports below).
What it adds that a fixed-geography breakdown cannot is a *flow-regime*
decomposition: because cloud regime correlates strongly with frontal/
baroclinic activity vs quiescent subsidence (Section 1.1), the cloud-regime
results in Section 1 should be read as the flow-regime analogue of the
storm-track-vs-tropics spatial split documented in the TUV synergy maps —
the same physical contrast (active baroclinic dynamics vs quiescent/
convective dynamics), viewed through cloud state rather than through
latitude or fixed geography. The qualitative agreement between the two
independent decompositions (FCN peaking in the most dynamically active
regime/zone in both cases; Aurora staying comparatively flat/uniform in
both) is itself evidence that these are not decomposition-specific artefacts
but a genuine property of how each model's temperature-insertion benefit is
organised by the real atmosphere's dynamical activity.


3. EXPERIMENT-LEVEL INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 What temperature-only insertion reveals, regime by regime.

Temperature insertion's benefit to the wind field (U500, V500) is positive
for every model and every regime except a few small FCN exceptions at the
Adjustment Phase (e.g. FCN V500 Clear adj = -0.72%) — i.e. the "wind is hard
to recover from temperature alone" asymmetry from the companion lats_FINAL
report (Section 1.1 there) holds regime-by-regime as well as latitude-by-
latitude: U500/V500 mature-phase gains here (5-46%) are consistently smaller
than the corresponding Z500 gains (20-95%) for every model in every regime.

3.2 Single-variable insertion helping one field while harming another.

The clearest case in this dataset is FCN: temperature insertion improves
FCN's Z500 (21-38% mature, every regime) while simultaneously degrading its
Q700 (-7.7% to -0.01% mature, every regime). The dynamical reading is that
FCN's smooth, large-scale spectral correction to temperature is well matched
to the smooth, large-scale geopotential field but is, if anything, slightly
counter-productive for the small-scale, sharply-varying, latent-heat-coupled
moisture field — the same mismatch documented in the companion TUV report
(Section 1.5) and discussed architecturally in Section 5 below.

3.3 Error propagation from the inserted variable into others.

Because temperature is the only variable forced to truth here, all of
Sections 1-2 are, by construction, measuring propagation. The cloud-regime
breakdown shows this propagation is itself regime-dependent and, for two of
the four models (FCN, GraphCast), can become regime-and-phase-dependent in
sign — i.e. propagation is not a fixed multiplier on the inserted variable's
accuracy but is conditioned on the local dynamical/cloud state, the forecast
phase, or both.


4. MODEL-BY-MODEL COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AURORA: best regions/regimes — uniformly strong across all cloud regimes for
Z500 (56-65% mature) and U500 (41-45% mature), with only a mild decline
toward the most heavily clouded (Opaque) regime; Q700 best in Clear (21.4%).
Weakest — V500 recovery is comparatively modest everywhere (20-28% mature)
and dips specifically in the transitional Cirrus/Mixed regimes (22.3%,
20.4%), suggesting the rotational wind component is hardest to recover from
temperature alone exactly in the regimes where the flow is itself
transitioning between balance states. Global vs local consistency: the
flattest, most regime-independent profile of the four models — evidence of
a genuinely general (not regime-specific) hydrostatic/thermal-wind coupling.

FOURCASTNET: best — a clean inverted-U peaking in the Mixed (frontal/
baroclinically active) regime for both Z500 (37.6%) and U500 (25.0%),
tracking dynamical activity rather than cloud amount per se (Section 1.2).
Weakest — moisture (Q700) is negative in every regime, the clearest and most
reproducible failure mode in this entire four-directory study (Section 1.5).
Global vs local consistency: smooth, monotonic-shaped regime dependence with
no sign flips in the wind/mass fields — consistent with a global, low-order
spectral representation (Section 5) that struggles specifically with the one
field (moisture) that is least smooth.

PRITHVI-WxC: best — the most regime-independent Z500 result of any model
(49-52% mature, every regime, a ~3-point spread — narrower than Aurora's
~10-point spread, though Aurora's magnitude is higher, see AURORA above);
Q700 improves specifically in the more actively clouded regimes (Section
1.3), though it is net-negative in Clear/Cirrus. Weakest — U500 and V500
are both modest across every regime (Section 1.3b), with U500 the only
cross-variable/model combination in this dataset that goes negative (Clear,
-4.8%) — continuing the wind/momentum weak point documented independently
in the companion lats_FINAL and tuv_final reports. Global vs local
consistency: strong local/vertical (thermodynamic) consistency,
regime-independent in shape though not the largest in magnitude; weaker
non-local (rotational) consistency, as established throughout this report
series.

GRAPHCAST: best — substantial short-term (Adjustment-Phase) gains in every
regime for every variable. Weakest — a systematic Adjustment-to-Mature
collapse for Z500 (Section 1.4), turning a positive short-term benefit into
a near-zero or negative one by the Mature Phase in three of four regimes;
this is the dataset's clearest illustration of a model whose response to
insertion is not durably absorbed into its own forward dynamics. Global vs
local consistency: the least temporally consistent of the four models —
its spatial/regime organisation looks reasonable at short lead time but does
not hold up as the forecast matures.


5. ARCHITECTURE AND TRAINING INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The cloud-regime results reinforce, with an independent flow-regime axis,
the architecture-linked tendencies already identified in the companion
lats_FINAL and tuv_final reports.

FCN's Adaptive Fourier Neural Operator (Pathak et al., 2022) represents the
field as a truncated set of global spectral (Fourier) modes. This is well
matched to representing a smooth, low-wavenumber field like Z500 (hence the
clean, monotonic-with-dynamical-activity inverted-U for FCN's Z500/U500
across cloud regimes) but poorly matched to a field like moisture, whose
true structure is dominated by sharp, small-scale, latent-heat-coupled
gradients that a low-order spectral truncation will tend to smooth away or
distort — consistent with FCN's reproducible Q700 degradation in every
single cloud regime tested. The broader NWP literature independently
supports treating cloud-conditioned fields as a distinct error regime:
cloud cover materially changes the statistics of temperature-forecast error
(larger Tmax/Tmin errors below ~40% cloud cover than under overcast skies;
Frontiers in Earth Science, 2023) and clouds are recognised as a leading
source of forecast error specifically because they index the predictability
of moist atmospheric flow — exactly the regime sensitivity this experiment
isolates for FCN's moisture pathway.

Aurora's 3D Swin-Transformer encoder-processor-decoder with multi-source,
multi-stage (including rollout-aware) pretraining (Bodnar et al., 2024/2025)
plausibly explains its flat, regime-independent strength: a foundation model
exposed to many heterogeneous training distributions and explicitly
fine-tuned for long rollouts would be expected to generalise more uniformly
across the different *local* dynamical/cloud regimes a single grid point can
pass through over a forecast, rather than specialising sharply to one regime
at the expense of another.

Prithvi-WxC's combination of masked-reconstruction and forecasting
pretraining on a patch-embedded 2D Vision Transformer (Schmude et al., 2024)
again explains the now-repeated pattern of strong local/instantaneous
thermodynamic coupling (Z500 from temperature) and weak non-local rotational
coupling (V500): a reconstruction objective rewards learning the
co-located, same-instant relationship between temperature and geopotential
very well, while providing comparatively little explicit pressure to learn
the spatially-extended, balance-constrained relationship between temperature
and the rotational wind field — and that asymmetry persists whether the
diagnostic axis used to expose it is latitude (lats_FINAL), domain mean
(tuv_final), or cloud regime (here).

GraphCast's icosahedral multi-mesh graph-neural-network architecture (Lam
et al., 2023) processes information through repeated local message-passing
steps across a fixed mesh hierarchy. A plausible explanation for its
Adjustment-to-Mature collapse (Section 1.4) is that the inserted information
is well used by the *encoder/processor* at the time of insertion (producing
a good Adjustment-Phase state) but is not "re-derived" or reinforced by the
model's own autoregressive evolution in the way Aurora's and FCN's
corrections evidently are — i.e. the correction is not anchored to a
self-sustaining internal balance, so it decays back toward GraphCast's own
intrinsic (less accurate) attractor as the rollout continues. This is
consistent with, though independent from, the equatorial breakdown documented
for GraphCast-S in the companion lats_FINAL report: both findings point to a
model whose corrections do not stay dynamically "locked in" outside the
specific regimes its message-passing mesh represents best.


6. FINAL SCIENTIFIC CONCLUSIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Cloud regime acts as a genuine, independent probe of the same
baroclinic-activity axis already identified by latitude in the companion
report — a model's response to temperature insertion is organised by local
dynamical activity, not simply by cloud amount.

(2) Most dynamically consistent model: Aurora, by virtue of its flat,
regime-independent strength across nearly all variables — the clearest sign
of a generalised, rather than regime-specialised, learned balance.

(3) Model benefiting most from insertion: Aurora, for the broadest,
strongest cross-variable benefit (Z500 56-65%, U500 41-45% mature, every
regime); Prithvi is a close second on Z500 specifically (49-52% mature,
the flattest regime profile of the four models) but its benefit is
narrowest overall (both wind components remain modest-to-negative in every
regime, Section 1.3b).

(4) Most important variable for correcting forecast evolution in this
dataset: temperature's effect on geopotential (hydrostatic pathway) is
strong and broadly regime-independent for three of four models; its effect
on the rotational wind field is weaker and more regime-sensitive for all
models, reconfirming the wind/mass asymmetry from the companion reports.

(5) Where the models fail physically: FCN's moisture field is reproducibly
and specifically harmed by temperature-only insertion in every cloud regime
tested — the single most consistent failure mode identified anywhere in
this four-part study; GraphCast's geopotential gains are not durable past
the Adjustment Phase in most regimes.

(6) Main takeaways for a paper/report: (a) cloud-regime stratification is a
cheap, physically motivated way to test whether a model's cross-variable
skill is organised by dynamical activity (it is, for FCN and Aurora) or is
largely regime-blind (Prithvi's Z500 pathway); (b) two structurally
different foundation models (Aurora, Prithvi) can extract the same kind of
useful temperature-moisture covariance from opposite ends of the cloud
spectrum, which is only visible with a regime-resolved analysis; and (c) a
model's Adjustment-Phase response to insertion is not a reliable predictor
of its Mature-Phase response (GraphCast), so insertion-based diagnostics of
AI weather models should always be evaluated across multiple forecast
phases, not just immediately after the perturbation is applied.
