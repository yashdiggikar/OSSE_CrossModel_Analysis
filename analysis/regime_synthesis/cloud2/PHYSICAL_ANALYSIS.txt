0. EXPERIMENT DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Wind (U+V) is inserted (forced to ERA5 truth) and the response of
temperature (T500), geopotential (Z500), and 850 hPa moisture (Q850) is
measured, stratified into the same four cloud regimes used in the companion
tcc report (Clear/Cirrus/Mixed/Opaque). This is the cloud-regime analogue of
the domain-mean Wind-Ins experiment already analysed in
common/lats_FINAL/PHYSICAL_ANALYSIS.txt and common/tuv_final/PHYSICAL_ANALYSIS.txt.

NOTE ON A NOW-FIXED DATA ISSUE — an earlier version of this report excluded
Prithvi's T500 row: the Control source file used for Prithvi in this script
previously pointed at the same OSSE output file used elsewhere as the
temperature-INSERTION run, so from the day-30 injection step onward its
T500 field was, by construction, equal to the ERA5 truth, producing an
artefactual exactly-0.0 "control" RMSE and hence an undefined percentage
improvement. Fixed 2026-07-14 by repointing Prithvi's control to the genuine
free-running control run (prithviOSSE/run2/output/control_forecast_Jan2022.nc)
and recomputing. All values in this report, including Prithvi's T500 row
below (Section 1.4), now come from that corrected control and are valid.


1. PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION OF MODEL BEHAVIOUR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 The wind-constrains-mass asymmetry holds, and is remarkably uniform
    across cloud regimes, for the two best-behaved models.

Aurora's T500 mature-phase improvement from wind insertion is 80-83% in
EVERY cloud regime (83.4% Clear, 80.4% Cirrus, 82.8% Mixed, 83.1% Opaque);
its Z500 improvement is 87-88% in every regime. FCN's T500 improvement
likewise stays in a narrow band (54-67% mature) with a mild peak in Mixed
(67.5%), and its Z500 improvement (70-85% mature) peaks in Mixed too (85.3%).
This reproduces, from an entirely different stratification axis, the same
two findings already established in the companion reports: (a) wind
information is a very strong, almost universally reliable constraint on the
mass/thermal field (the geostrophic/thermal-wind pathway), and (b) FCN's
sensitivity to the local dynamical regime (peaking specifically in Mixed,
the most actively baroclinic/frontal regime) versus Aurora's comparatively
flat, regime-independent strength — exactly the same model-specific
signature documented for the reverse experiment (temperature insertion) in
the companion tcc report (Section 1.2 there).

1.2 Moisture (Q850) is the field where cloud regime matters MOST, and where
    the four models disagree most sharply in sign.

Aurora's Q850 response to wind insertion shows a U-shape across regimes:
strongest in the two "pure" end-member regimes (47.2% Clear, 38.2% Opaque)
and weakest in the two transitional regimes (23.0% Cirrus, 22.8% Mixed).
Physically, Clear and Opaque are the most homogeneous, large-scale-organised
moisture states (respectively dry/subsiding and saturated/deeply-lifted),
while Cirrus and Mixed are transitional states where moisture is patchier
and less directly tied to the large-scale circulation alone — exactly where
a purely wind-driven (advective) correction would be expected to be least
sufficient on its own. FCN's Q850 response is comparatively flat (36-40%
across all regimes) — FCN does not show this U-shape, suggesting its
moisture-from-wind pathway, whatever its limitations (Section 1.3, tcc
report), is not as strongly regime-conditioned as Aurora's.

GraphCast and Prithvi diverge sharply, and in OPPOSITE directions, by the
Mature Phase. GraphCast's Q850 Adjustment-Phase response is strongly
positive in every regime (29-44%) but collapses by the Mature Phase to
near-zero or negative in three of four regimes (2.7% Cirrus, -1.3% Mixed,
6.4% Opaque, with only Clear remaining strong at 45.9%) — the same
Adjustment-to-Mature collapse documented for GraphCast's Z500/T500 pathway
in the companion tcc report (Section 1.4) and, again, in the terrain2 report
below, now confirmed a third time. Prithvi's Q850 response is, uniquely,
NEGATIVE at the Mature Phase in every single regime (-19.3% Clear, -15.4%
Cirrus, -11.7% Mixed, -10.6% Opaque) despite being mildly positive or
near-zero at the Adjustment Phase — i.e. wind insertion actively and
increasingly degrades Prithvi's moisture forecast the longer the model runs,
in every cloud regime tested.

1.3 Z500's response to wind insertion is the cleanest single confirmation in
    this report series of Prithvi's selective wind/momentum weak point.

Prithvi's Z500 improvement from wind insertion is essentially zero across
every regime (7.2%, 3.2%, -0.2%, 0.1% Adjustment; 0.3%, 3.5%, 5.6%, 7.9%
Mature) — by far the weakest Z500 response to ANY insertion, by ANY model,
anywhere in this four-report series (compare to Prithvi's own 49-52% Z500
response to temperature insertion in the tcc report, Section 1.3 there —
itself far larger than the ~0% response to wind insertion found here).
Since Z500 here is being driven purely by the wind field (the directly
inserted variable), this is unambiguous: Prithvi's internal representation
does not translate an accurate wind field into an improved geopotential
field, in any cloud regime, at any phase — the clearest possible
confirmation that Prithvi's weak point is specifically the wind-to-mass
(geostrophic) coupling direction, not a general insensitivity to insertion.

1.4 Prithvi's temperature field is also actively harmed by wind insertion —
    every cloud regime, and worsening over time — extending its Q850
    failure (Section 1.2) to a second non-inserted field.

Prithvi's T500 mature-phase "improvement" from wind insertion is negative in
every cloud regime (-29.2% Clear, -14.0% Cirrus, -16.9% Mixed, -23.2%
Opaque), and is already negative at the Adjustment Phase in every regime too
(-7.1% to -10.0%). Unlike Q850 (Section 1.2), which starts near-neutral-to-
mildly-positive at the Adjustment Phase before turning negative, Prithvi's
T500 response is harmful from the very first phase and grows more harmful as
the forecast matures. Combined with Section 1.3's near-zero Z500 response,
this completes the picture: an artificially perfect wind field measurably
degrades two of Prithvi's three examined cross-variables (T500, Q850) and
does essentially nothing for the third (Z500), in every cloud regime tested —
the most comprehensive single piece of evidence in this report series that
Prithvi's wind representation is not merely disconnected from the rest of
its state but, once forced to an out-of-distribution "perfect" value,
actively destabilises it.


2. REGIONAL AND SPATIAL ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
As in the companion tcc report, this dataset's stratification is by
instantaneous local cloud/flow regime rather than fixed geography, so it
should be read as a flow-regime decomposition rather than a literal map.
Read alongside the TUV spatial synergy maps (common/tuv_final/PHYSICAL_ANALYSIS.txt,
Section 2), the cloud-regime results here corroborate the same physical
picture from a different angle: Aurora's near-saturation of the wind-to-mass
pathway (uniform 80-88% across regimes) is consistent with the TUV maps
showing Aurora's Z500 already very well constrained by wind almost
everywhere except over a handful of specific subtropical desert/thermal
regions; FCN's regime-peaked (Mixed-favoured) profile is consistent with the
TUV maps showing FCN's positive synergy concentrated along the storm
tracks. The moisture U-shape found for Aurora here (Section 1.2) is also
broadly consistent with the TUV maps' finding that Aurora's moisture synergy
is strongly positive over open ocean (a relatively homogeneous, Clear- or
Opaque-leaning moisture source region) and weaker/negative over cold,
dry continental interiors.


3. EXPERIMENT-LEVEL INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 What wind-only insertion reveals about each model's dependence
    structure, regime by regime.

For Aurora and FCN, wind insertion is a strong, broadly regime-independent
(Aurora) or mildly regime-dependent (FCN) constraint on both temperature and
geopotential — confirming the thermal-wind/geostrophic relation is well
represented internally for both models across essentially the whole
dynamical spectrum sampled by these four cloud regimes. For Prithvi, wind
insertion is a near-total non-event for Z500 (Section 1.3) and an actively
harmful one for Q850 (Section 1.2) — wind information does not propagate
constructively into either the mass field or the moisture field for this
model, in this experiment.

3.2 Cases where wind insertion helps one field but harms another.

The single clearest case in this dataset is Prithvi: U/V is, by
construction, made perfectly accurate, yet both Q850 (Section 1.2) and T500
(Section 1.4) get WORSE in every regime by the Mature Phase, while Z500 is
left essentially unchanged (Section 1.3). The likely dynamical reading,
consistent with the companion reports' findings on Prithvi, is that an
artificially accurate wind field, decoupled from the rest of Prithvi's
internal representation, introduces a state the model's decoder cannot
reconcile with its own (uncorrected) thermodynamic and moisture fields, and
the inconsistency manifests as amplifying error in both rather than damping
it — the same kind of "insertion as out-of-distribution forcing" failure
mode documented for Prithvi's temperature field under Wind-Ins in the
companion lats_FINAL report (Section 1.4 there).

3.3 GraphCast: a third confirmation of the Adjustment-to-Mature collapse.

GraphCast's Z500/T500/Q850 all show the same qualitative pattern here: large
positive Adjustment-Phase gains that erode substantially, and sometimes
reverse, by the Mature Phase, in nearly every cloud regime. Because this
pattern now appears independently in three different analyses (tcc, cloud2,
and terrain2 below) and for both single-variable insertion experiments
(T-only and UV-only), it should be treated as a robust, general property of
GraphCast's response to insertion in this study, not an artefact of any one
experimental design.


4. MODEL-BY-MODEL COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AURORA: best — T500 and Z500 respond strongly and almost identically across
all four cloud regimes (80-88% mature), the most regime-independent
wind-to-mass coupling of any model in this dataset. Weakest — Q850 dips in
the two transitional regimes (Cirrus, Mixed), the model's only meaningfully
regime-sensitive cross-variable pathway here. Global vs local consistency:
highly globally consistent — the same strong pathway operates regardless of
local cloud state.

FOURCASTNET: best — T500/Z500 both peak in the Mixed (most actively
baroclinic) regime, the cleanest "skill tracks dynamical activity" signature
of the four models, mirroring its own behaviour under temperature insertion
(tcc report, Section 1.2). Weakest — none of FCN's fields go negative here
(unlike the tcc report's moisture finding), making wind insertion a more
uniformly safe constraint for FCN than temperature insertion is. Global vs
local consistency: moderate regime-dependence, smoothly varying, no sign
flips — consistent with a global spectral representation.

PRITHVI-WxC: best — none of the three cross-variables examined here show a
strong, reliable positive response to wind insertion (T500 negative in
every regime, -14% to -29% mature; Z500 ~0%; Q850 negative in every
regime). This experiment is, on its own, the strongest single piece of
evidence in this report series that Prithvi's wind field is dynamically
disconnected from — and, once forced, actively destabilising to — its mass,
thermal, and moisture fields alike. Weakest — T500 and Q850 are both
actively and increasingly harmed in every regime, T500 slightly more
severely (up to -29.2% Clear) than Q850 (up to -19.3% Clear). Global vs
local consistency: consistently harmful across regimes for two of three
fields — a rare case where "consistency" itself is the negative finding.

GRAPHCAST: best — large short-term (Adjustment-Phase) gains across every
field and regime. Weakest — the Mature-Phase collapse documented in Section
1.2, the clearest and most consistently reproduced failure mode for this
model across this entire four-part study. Global vs local consistency:
the model's regime organisation looks broadly sensible (no equatorial-style
sign flips here, unlike the lats_FINAL dataset) but its TEMPORAL consistency
is the weakest of the four models.


5. ARCHITECTURE AND TRAINING INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This section reinforces, rather than repeats, the architectural discussion
in the companion lats_FINAL, tuv_final, and tcc reports.

Aurora's foundation-model pretraining across heterogeneous, multi-source
geophysical data, combined with explicit rollout-aware fine-tuning (Bodnar
et al., 2024/2025), is again the most natural explanation for why its
wind-to-mass pathway is essentially saturated and regime-independent here:
broad training exposure plausibly teaches the model a single, robust,
generally-applicable form of the geostrophic/thermal-wind relation rather
than a regime-specific one.

FCN's global spectral (AFNO) representation (Pathak et al., 2022) explains
why its skill specifically tracks the most actively baroclinic regime
(Mixed) — a global low-order spectral operator is naturally well suited to
representing the large-scale, wave-like structures associated with active
frontal/baroclinic systems, and somewhat less suited to the comparatively
featureless Clear regime or the small-scale, latent-heat-dominated structure
of the Opaque regime.

Prithvi-WxC's masked-reconstruction-plus-forecasting pretraining on a
patch-embedded 2D Vision Transformer (Schmude et al., 2024) is, by this
point in the report series, a well-supported explanation for the model's
specific and repeated failure to couple wind information into either the
mass field (Z500, Section 1.3) or the moisture field (Q850, Section 1.2):
a reconstruction objective built around co-located, same-instant variable
relationships provides little training signal for the spatially-extended,
balance-mediated relationship between a forced wind field and the rest of
the atmospheric state, so when wind actually IS the forced/inserted
variable, the model has comparatively little learned machinery to exploit
it.

GraphCast's icosahedral multi-mesh message-passing architecture (Lam et al.,
2023) is consistent with the now-repeated Adjustment-to-Mature collapse
pattern: a graph-based encoder/processor can incorporate the inserted
information effectively into a single forward pass (producing a good
Adjustment-Phase state) without that information being durably re-derived by
the model's own autoregressive evolution, so the benefit fades as the
forecast continues to roll out under the model's own, less-corrected,
dynamics.


6. FINAL SCIENTIFIC CONCLUSIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Physical understanding revealed: wind insertion is, for Aurora and FCN,
a near-universally reliable, regime-spanning constraint on the mass and
thermal fields — direct evidence both models have learned a broadly
applicable form of geostrophic/thermal-wind balance. For Prithvi, the same
experiment reveals the opposite: wind information is largely inert for the
mass field and actively counter-productive for BOTH temperature and
moisture, across every cloud regime tested.

(2) Most dynamically consistent model: Aurora — the flattest, strongest,
most regime-independent response of the four.

(3) Model benefiting most from insertion: FCN, in the sense that its
response, while regime-dependent, is uniformly positive (no field goes
negative anywhere in this dataset) — a smaller peak benefit than Aurora's
but a more uniformly safe one across the board.

(4) Most important variable: wind, for both Aurora and FCN, in correcting
temperature and geopotential simultaneously — confirming, from this new
flow-regime axis, the central wind-to-mass pathway already established in
the companion reports.

(5) Where the models fail physically: Prithvi's wind field is dynamically
disconnected from its mass field and actively harmful to its thermal and
moisture fields, in every cloud regime — the most unambiguous demonstration
of this specific weak point in the entire four-report series; GraphCast's
gains do not survive into the Mature Phase.

(6) Main takeaways for a paper/report: (a) cloud-regime-resolved insertion
experiments can isolate a model's weakest cross-variable pathway with more
precision than a domain-mean experiment alone, because a uniformly weak or
harmful response across every regime (as found here for Prithvi) rules out
the possibility that the domain-mean result was being driven by one
unusual region or regime; (b) moisture is the field most sensitive to cloud
regime for every model, consistent with the broader NWP literature treating
cloud state as a first-order conditioning variable for forecast error; and
(c) GraphCast's Adjustment-to-Mature collapse, now observed in three
independent analyses in this series, should be treated as a structural
property of the model's response to insertion, worth investigating
architecturally (e.g. whether its message-passing processor under-weights
self-consistency across rollout steps) rather than as noise.
